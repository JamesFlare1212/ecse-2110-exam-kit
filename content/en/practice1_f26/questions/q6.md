## Question

A 440-V, 3-phase voltage feeds a balanced delta-connected 3-phase load with an impedance of $(25+j10)\,\Omega$/phase

## Guide

Each delta branch receives the full line-to-line voltage. Find branch currents first, then subtract the two currents meeting at each line terminal. State the phase sequence and reference before assigning line-current angles.

## Part a

Calculate the line current and represent in phasor form

### Solution

Use the reference sheet's [delta line-current relation](formula:delta) and phasor Ohm's law:

$$
I_{ab}=\frac{V_{ab}}{Z_\Delta},\qquad I_a=I_{ab}-I_{ca}
$$

Choose positive sequence with $V_{ab}=440\angle0^\circ$, $V_{bc}=440\angle-120^\circ$ and $V_{ca}=440\angle120^\circ$ V. The load impedance is $Z_\Delta=25+j10=26.9258\angle21.8014^\circ\,\Omega$. Thus

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{ab}&=\frac{440}{25+j10}=15.1724-j6.06897=16.3412\angle-21.8014^\circ\,\mathrm A\\
I_{ca}&=16.3412\angle98.1986^\circ=-2.33040+j16.1741\,\mathrm A\\
I_a&=(15.1724-j6.06897)-(-2.33040+j16.1741)\\
&=17.5028-j22.2431\,\mathrm A
\end{aligned}
$$

The other two line currents are displaced by $\mp120^\circ$:

$$
\boxed{I_a=28.3038\angle-51.8014^\circ\,\mathrm A,\quad
I_b=28.3038\angle-171.8014^\circ\,\mathrm A,\quad
I_c=28.3038\angle68.1986^\circ\,\mathrm A}
$$

## Part b

Calculate the power absorbed per phase and

### Solution

Use Lecture 2, page 13's complex-power definition for one delta branch:

$$
P_\phi=\operatorname{Re}(V_{ab}I_{ab}^*)=|I_{ab}|^2R=\frac{|V_{ab}|^2R}{R^2+X^2}
$$

The branch voltage is 440 V RMS, with $R=25\,\Omega$ and $X=10\,\Omega$. Substitute these values without confusing apparent power with real power:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_\phi&=\operatorname{Re}\{440(15.1724+j6.06897)\}\\
&=\frac{440^2(25)}{25^2+10^2}=\boxed{6675.86\,\mathrm W\simeq6.67586\,\mathrm{kW}}
\end{aligned}
$$

### Source correction

PDF page 8 reports 7190.2 W per phase. The displayed complex multiplication actually gives 6675.86 W; approximately 7190 VA is the branch's apparent power, not its real power. The source's intermediate branch names and signs are inconsistent, so part (a) explicitly defines a standard positive sequence instead of inheriting those labels.

## Part c

Calculate the sum of all phase currents. Explain why this sum has such a value

### Solution

Balanced equal impedances give three branch-current phasors with equal magnitude and $120^\circ$ separation. Factoring out $I_{ab}$ gives

$$
I_{ab}+I_{bc}+I_{ca}=I_{ab}\left(1+e^{-j120^\circ}+e^{j120^\circ}\right)
$$

The real components of the two rotated unit phasors are $-1/2$, while their imaginary components are opposite. Therefore

$$
I_{ab}+I_{bc}+I_{ca}
=I_{ab}\left[1+\left(-\tfrac12-j\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)+\left(-\tfrac12+j\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)\right]
=\boxed{0\,\mathrm A}
$$

This is a phasor sum, not a sum of magnitudes. The balanced sinusoidal instantaneous currents also sum to zero at every time. If “phase currents” is intended to mean the three line currents, their sum is also zero: $I_a+I_b+I_c=(I_{ab}-I_{ca})+(I_{bc}-I_{ab})+(I_{ca}-I_{bc})$ by Kirchhoff's current law.
