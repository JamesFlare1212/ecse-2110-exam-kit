## Question

A single-phase load is supplied with a sinusoidal voltage

$$
v(t)=200\cos(377t)
$$

The resulting instantaneous power is

$$
p(t)=800+1000\cos(754t-36.87^\circ)
$$

## Guide

Expand the double-frequency cosine and compare it with the instantaneous-power relation for sinusoidal voltage and current. Distinguish the voltage's $200\,\mathrm V$ peak from its RMS value before using complex power or Ohm's law.

## Part a

Find the complex power supplied to the load.

### Solution

Lecture 2, pages 7 and 13 gives instantaneous power and RMS complex power. With voltage phase zero and current lag $\theta$,

$$
p(t)=VI\cos\theta+VI\cos(2\omega t-\theta),\qquad S=VI^*=P+jQ
$$

Here $V,I$ in the scalar product denote RMS magnitudes. The stated ripple amplitude is $VI=1000\,\mathrm{VA}$ and $\theta=36.87^\circ$. Expand the given ripple to identify its in-phase and quadrature coefficients:

$$
\begin{aligned}
p(t)&=800+1000\cos36.87^\circ\cos754t+1000\sin36.87^\circ\sin754t\\
&\simeq800+800\cos754t+600\sin754t
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus the average power is the constant $800\,\mathrm W$ and the positive sine coefficient gives inductive reactive power:

$$
S=P+jQ\simeq\boxed{800+j600\,\mathrm{VA}=1000\angle36.87^\circ\,\mathrm{VA}}
$$

### Source correction

PDF page 1 says “$P=600\,\mathrm W$” immediately before writing $S=800+j600$. The average of the given instantaneous power is $800\,\mathrm W$, so the prose value is a typo. The printed angle $36.87^\circ$ is rounded: treating every printed decimal as exact gives a tiny inconsistency between $1000\cos36.87^\circ$ and $800$. The results use the intended $800$–$600$–$1000$ power triangle, at the source's precision.

## Part b

Find the instantaneous current $i(t)$ and the rms value of the current supplied to the load.

### Solution

Use the [sinusoidal RMS–peak relation](formula:rms-peak) and Lecture 2, page 13's complex-power definition:

$$
V=\frac{V_m}{\sqrt2}\angle0^\circ,\qquad I=\left(\frac{S}{V}\right)^*
$$

The voltage peak is $V_m=200\,\mathrm V$. Substitute the complex power from part (a):

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=\frac{200}{\sqrt2}=141.421\,\mathrm V\\
I&=\frac{800-j600}{141.421}=5.65685-j4.24264\,\mathrm A\\
&=7.07107\angle-36.87^\circ\,\mathrm A,\qquad I_m=\sqrt2(7.07107)=10\,\mathrm A
\end{aligned}
$$

Returning to the time domain gives

$$
\boxed{i(t)=10\cos(377t-36.87^\circ)\,\mathrm A,\qquad I_{\mathrm{rms}}=7.07107\,\mathrm A}
$$

## Part c

Find the load impedance.

### Solution

Use phasor Ohm's law, $Z=V/I$, with the RMS phasors calculated in part (b):

$$
Z=\frac{141.421\angle0^\circ}{7.07107\angle-36.87^\circ}\,\Omega
$$

The magnitude ratio is $20$ and the phase difference is positive $36.87^\circ$. Resolve the polar impedance into its resistive and reactive components:

$$
Z=20\bigl(\cos36.87^\circ+j\sin36.87^\circ\bigr)\,\Omega
\simeq\boxed{16+j12\,\Omega=20\angle36.87^\circ\,\Omega}
$$
