## Question

A $1\phi$, $10\,\mathrm{kVA}$, $2400/240\,\mathrm V$, $60\,\mathrm{Hz}$ distribution transformer has the following characteristics:

- Core loss at full voltage $=100\,\mathrm W$
- Copper loss at half load $=60\,\mathrm W$

## Guide

Convert the half-load copper loss to full-load loss before solving any part. For maximum efficiency hold voltage and power factor fixed and vary the load fraction. For all-day efficiency add energy, including core loss during the six hours with no output.

## Part a

Determine the efficiency of the transformer when it delivers full load at $0.8$ power factor lagging.

### Solution

Use the reference sheet's [transformer efficiency](formula:transformer-efficiency) with $P_{cu}(x)=x^2P_{cu,N}$:

$$
\eta=\frac{P_{\mathrm{out}}}{P_{\mathrm{out}}+P_c+P_{cu}},\qquad P_{\mathrm{out}}=xS_N\,\mathrm{pf}
$$

Given $P_c=100\,\mathrm W$ and half-load copper loss $60\,\mathrm W$, full-load copper loss is

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{cu,N}&=\frac{60}{0.5^2}=240\,\mathrm W\\
P_{\mathrm{out}}&=1(10000)(0.8)=8000\,\mathrm W
\end{aligned}
$$

At full load the efficiency is

$$
\eta=\frac{8000}{8000+100+240}\times100\%=\boxed{95.9233\%}
$$

## Part b

Determine the per-unit rating at which the transformer efficiency is a maximum. Determine this efficiency if the load power factor is $0.9$.

### Solution

The efficiency expression from part (a) can be differentiated to obtain Lecture 7, page 9's maximum-efficiency condition. At fixed power factor define $A=S_N\,\mathrm{pf}$ and $k=P_{cu,N}$:

$$
\eta(x)=\frac{Ax}{Ax+P_c+kx^2},\qquad
\eta'(x)=\frac{A(P_c-kx^2)}{(Ax+P_c+kx^2)^2}
$$

For $A>0$, the derivative changes from positive to negative when $kx^2=P_c$. With $k=240\,\mathrm W$, $P_c=100\,\mathrm W$, and power factor $0.9$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{\max}&=\sqrt{\frac{100}{240}}=0.645497\\
P_{cu}(x_{\max})&=100\,\mathrm W\\
P_{\mathrm{out}}&=10000(0.645497)(0.9)=5809.475\,\mathrm W
\end{aligned}
$$

The requested per-unit loading and efficiency are

$$
\boxed{x_{\max}=0.645497\,\mathrm{pu},\qquad
\eta_{\max}=\frac{5809.475}{5809.475+100+100}\times100\%=96.6719\%}
$$

## Part c

The transformer has the following load cycle:

- No load for $6\,\mathrm{hours}$
- $70\%$ full load for $10\,\mathrm{hours}$ at $0.8$ PF
- $90\%$ full load for $8\,\mathrm{hours}$ at $0.9$ PF

Determine the all-day efficiency of the transformer.

### Solution

Lecture 7, page 17 defines all-day efficiency as output energy divided by input energy. In the usual constant-voltage loss model,

$$
\eta_{\mathrm{day}}=\frac{E_{\mathrm{out}}}{E_{\mathrm{out}}+E_c+E_{cu}},\qquad
E_{\mathrm{out}}=\sum xS_N\,\mathrm{pf}\,t,\qquad E_{cu}=\sum x^2P_{cu,N}t
$$

The stated intervals sum to $24\,\mathrm{hours}$. Assume the transformer remains energized at rated voltage during all three intervals; no-load copper loss is neglected in this approximate model. Using $\mathrm{kW}$ and $\mathrm{hours}$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{\mathrm{out}}&=0(6)+10(0.7)(0.8)(10)+10(0.9)(0.9)(8)\\
&=56+64.8=120.8\,\mathrm{kWh}\\
E_c&=0.100(6+10+8)=2.4\,\mathrm{kWh}\\
E_{cu}&=0.240\left[0^2(6)+0.7^2(10)+0.9^2(8)\right]\\
&=0.240(4.9+6.48)=2.7312\,\mathrm{kWh}
\end{aligned}
$$

Substitute the total energies, rather than averaging the interval efficiencies:

$$
\eta_{\mathrm{day}}=\frac{120.8}{120.8+2.4+2.7312}\times100\%=\boxed{95.9254\%}
$$
