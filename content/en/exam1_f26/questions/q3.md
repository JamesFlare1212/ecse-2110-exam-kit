## Question

Describe the Aluminum Conductor with Steel Reinforcement (ACSR) conductor and justify its structure.

## Guide

Use the ACSR photograph in Lecture 1, PDF page 19, to identify the central reinforcement and surrounding strands. Explain which material primarily carries current and which carries tensile load, then relate that arrangement to an overhead span. The official description is on solution PDF page 7.

## Part a

Describe the Aluminum Conductor with Steel Reinforcement (ACSR) conductor and justify its structure.

### Solution

Use the basic conductor-resistance relation to compare the electrical role of parallel longitudinal strands; this is a material/circuit relation, not a formula printed on a course formula sheet:

$$
R=\rho\frac{\ell}{A},\qquad I_k=\frac{V}{R_k},\qquad I_{\mathrm{total}}=\sum_k I_k.
$$

No dimensions or material resistivities are given, so a structural explanation is required. ACSR has a solid or stranded galvanized steel core surrounded by one or more layers of stranded aluminum. Aluminum provides a large conductive cross-section at low weight and cost; steel provides tensile strength to withstand conductor weight, wind and ice over long spans. Galvanizing protects the steel against corrosion, and stranding permits flexibility.

For the same longitudinal voltage, the more conductive aluminum paths carry most of the current. As a simple DC or uniform-current comparison with equal strand lengths,

$$
\frac{I_{\mathrm{Al}}}{I_{\mathrm{steel}}}
=\frac{R_{\mathrm{steel}}}{R_{\mathrm{Al}}}
=\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{steel}}A_{\mathrm{Al}}}{\rho_{\mathrm{Al}}A_{\mathrm{steel}}}.
$$

The official answer also mentions skin effect. AC current crowding toward the outside is compatible with placing aluminum outside, but the skin effect at power frequency is size dependent: it does not make the steel current exactly zero or replace the main conductivity/strength justification. The required division of roles is

$$
\boxed{\text{Outer aluminum strands: chiefly electrical conduction and low mass;}\qquad \text{central steel core: chiefly mechanical reinforcement.}}
$$

### Source correction

The handwritten response in the scanned question PDF overstates that the core carries very little current solely because of skin effect. The official solution’s reference to skin effect is qualitative. Actual AC current sharing also depends on strand arrangement, steel permeability and conductor diameter; the aluminum/steel material roles do not require assuming zero core current.
