## Question

A $1\phi$, $25\,\mathrm{kVA}$, $220/440\,\mathrm V$, $60\,\mathrm{Hz}$ transformer gave the following test results.

- Open circuit test ($440\,\mathrm V$ side open): $220\,\mathrm V$, $9.5\,\mathrm A$, $650\,\mathrm W$
- Short-circuit test ($220\,\mathrm V$ side shorted): $37.5\,\mathrm V$, $55\,\mathrm A$, $950\,\mathrm W$

## Guide

Identify the energized winding before using each test: the open-circuit measurements are on the low-voltage side, and the short-circuit measurements are on the high-voltage side. Extract the shunt and series branches independently, then divide each by the impedance base for its own side. Use the common per-unit circuit to compute full-load regulation and construct the lagging-load phasors.

## Part a

Derive the approximate equivalent circuit in per-unit values.

### Solution

Use the test relations from Lecture 6, PDF page 8. The open-circuit power models core loss; short-circuit power models series copper loss, neglecting the small associated corrections:

$$
R_c=\frac{V_{\mathrm{oc}}^2}{P_{\mathrm{oc}}},\quad I_c=\frac{P_{\mathrm{oc}}}{V_{\mathrm{oc}}},\quad I_m=\sqrt{I_{\mathrm{oc}}^2-I_c^2},\quad X_m=\frac{V_{\mathrm{oc}}}{I_m},\qquad R_{\mathrm{eq}}=\frac{P_{\mathrm{sc}}}{I_{\mathrm{sc}}^2},\quad |Z_{\mathrm{eq}}|=\frac{V_{\mathrm{sc}}}{I_{\mathrm{sc}}},\quad X_{\mathrm{eq}}=\sqrt{|Z_{\mathrm{eq}}|^2-R_{\mathrm{eq}}^2}.
$$

The $440\,\mathrm V$ winding is open in the first test, so $220\,\mathrm V$, $9.5\,\mathrm A$, and $650\,\mathrm W$ are measured on the low-voltage side. Substitution gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{c,L}&=\frac{220^2}{650}=74.46154\,\Omega,\qquad I_{c,L}=\frac{650}{220}=2.954545\,\mathrm A,\\
I_{m,L}&=\sqrt{9.5^2-(650/220)^2}=9.028879\,\mathrm A,\\
X_{m,L}&=\frac{220}{9.028879}=24.36626\,\Omega.
\end{aligned}
$$

The $220\,\mathrm V$ winding is shorted in the second test, so $37.5\,\mathrm V$, $55\,\mathrm A$, and $950\,\mathrm W$ are measured on the high-voltage side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{eq},H}&=\frac{950}{55^2}=0.3140496\,\Omega,\\
|Z_{\mathrm{eq},H}|&=\frac{37.5}{55}=0.6818182\,\Omega,\\
X_{\mathrm{eq},H}&=\sqrt{(37.5/55)^2-(950/55^2)^2}=0.6051850\,\Omega.
\end{aligned}
$$

Use the single-phase rated bases $S_b=25000\,\mathrm{VA}$, $V_{b,L}=220\,\mathrm V$, and $V_{b,H}=440\,\mathrm V$. From $I_b=S_b/V_b$ and $Z_b=V_b^2/S_b$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{b,L}&=25000/220=113.6364\,\mathrm A,& Z_{b,L}&=220^2/25000=1.936\,\Omega,\\
I_{b,H}&=25000/440=56.81818\,\mathrm A,& Z_{b,H}&=440^2/25000=7.744\,\Omega.
\end{aligned}
$$

Per-unit normalization is defined in Lecture 6, PDF page 13. Divide each branch by the impedance base of its measurement side. Equivalently, refer the high-voltage series impedance to the low side with $(220/440)^2=1/4$ and divide by $1.936\,\Omega$; both routes give the same per-unit circuit. The approximate circuit has $R_c$ and $jX_m$ in parallel at the input and $r_{\mathrm{eq}}+jx_{\mathrm{eq}}$ in series with the load:

![Per-unit transformer circuit and branch correspondence](figure:e1-f26-q6-equivalent)

$$
\boxed{R_{c,\mathrm{pu}}=\frac{74.46154}{1.936}=38.46154,\quad X_{m,\mathrm{pu}}=\frac{24.36626}{1.936}=12.58588,\quad z_{\mathrm{eq,pu}}=\frac{0.3140496+j0.6051850}{7.744}=0.0405539+j0.0781489.}
$$

### Source correction

Use the test sides exactly as printed: open circuit on the $440\,\mathrm V$ winding means the $220\,\mathrm V$ winding is energized; short circuit on the $220\,\mathrm V$ winding means the $440\,\mathrm V$ winding is energized. The handwritten calculations in the question scan mix test symbols and powers and must not be treated as corrected givens. The official solution’s handwritten resistance denominator appears as $53^2$, although the printed test current is $55\,\mathrm A$ and its numerical result $0.314\,\Omega$ corresponds to $950/55^2$, not $950/53^2$. The stated $55\,\mathrm A$ is retained here. Its side assignments are consistent, but its early rounding to $|Z_{\mathrm{eq},H}|=0.68\,\Omega$ leads to $X_{\mathrm{eq},H}\approx0.603\,\Omega$ and $x_{\mathrm{eq}}\approx0.0779$; retaining the data gives $0.605185\,\Omega$ and $0.0781489$.

The measured short-circuit current $55\,\mathrm A$ is not exactly the rated high-side current $56.81818\,\mathrm A$. Use $55\,\mathrm A$ to extract impedance, then the rated current for a full-load calculation. In particular, $950\,\mathrm W$ is the copper loss at the test current, not precisely the full-load copper loss; scaling by $(56.81818/55)^2$ gives about $1013.85\,\mathrm W$.

## Part b

Determine the voltage regulation at full load, $0.8$ PF lagging.

### Solution

Use the approximate equivalent-circuit phasor equation from Lecture 6, PDF page 6, and the regulation definition from page 10, all on matching per-unit bases:

$$
v_1=v_2'+i_2'z_{\mathrm{eq}},\qquad \mathrm{VR}=\frac{|v_1|-|v_2'|}{|v_2'|}\,100\%.
$$

Take rated load terminal voltage as reference, $v_2'=1\angle0^\circ$, and full-load current magnitude as $1\,\mathrm{pu}$. At $0.8$ lagging power factor, the current angle is $-\cos^{-1}(0.8)=-36.8699^\circ$, hence $i_2'=0.8-j0.6$. Using the parameters from (a),

$$
\begin{aligned}
i_2'z_{\mathrm{eq}}&=(0.8-j0.6)(0.0405539239+j0.0781488901)\\
&=(0.8\cdot0.0405539239+0.6\cdot0.0781488901)\\
&\quad+j(0.8\cdot0.0781488901-0.6\cdot0.0405539239)\\
&=0.07933247+j0.03818676,\\
v_1&=1.07933247+j0.03818676,\\
|v_1|&=\sqrt{1.07933247^2+0.03818676^2}=1.080007785.
\end{aligned}
$$

This is the magnitude calculation within the approximate circuit, which neglects the small exciting-current contribution to series drop. The often-used first-order approximation keeps only the in-phase drop, $100(r\cos\theta+x\sin\theta)=7.93325\%$; retaining the quadrature drop gives

$$
\boxed{v_1=1.0800078\angle2.02628^\circ\ \mathrm{pu},\qquad \mathrm{VR}=\frac{1.080007785-1}{1}\,100\%=8.00078\%\approx8.00\%.}
$$

## Part c

Draw the phasor diagram for condition (b).

### Solution

Use the same equivalent-circuit voltage sum from Lecture 6, PDF page 6, resolving the impedance drop into resistive and reactive components:

$$
v_1=v_2'+i_2'r_{\mathrm{eq}}+ji_2'x_{\mathrm{eq}}.
$$

The condition from (b) is $v_2'=1\angle0^\circ$ and $i_2'=1\angle-36.8699^\circ$. Draw the load voltage along the positive real axis and the current below it. The resistive drop is parallel to the current; the reactive drop leads that current by $90^\circ$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
i_2'r_{\mathrm{eq}}&=(0.8-j0.6)(0.0405539239)=0.03244314-j0.02433235,\\
ji_2'x_{\mathrm{eq}}&=j(0.8-j0.6)(0.0781488901)=0.04688933+j0.06251911,\\
v_2'+i_2'r_{\mathrm{eq}}&=1.03244314-j0.02433235,\\
v_1&=(1.03244314-j0.02433235)+(0.04688933+j0.06251911)\\
&=1.07933247+j0.03818676.
\end{aligned}
$$

![Voltage phasors and enlarged drop construction](figure:e1-f26-q6-phasor)

The solution figure joins the voltage drops head-to-tail and shows their enlarged construction separately. The input-voltage arrow ends above the real axis; the current arrow is drawn on its own scale. These are the defining angles and endpoint:

$$
\boxed{\arg i_2'=-36.8699^\circ,\quad \arg(i_2'r_{\mathrm{eq}})=-36.8699^\circ,\quad \arg(ji_2'x_{\mathrm{eq}})=53.1301^\circ,\quad v_1=1.0800078\angle2.02628^\circ\ \mathrm{pu}.}
$$
