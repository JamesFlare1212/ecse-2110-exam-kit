## Question

A balanced $\Delta$-connected load consisting of a pure resistances of $18\,\Omega$ per phase in parallel with a purely resistive balanced $Y$-connected load of $12\,\Omega$ per phase as shown in Figure. The combination is connected to a three-phase balanced supply of $346.41\text{-}\mathrm{V}$ rms (line-to-line) via a three-phase line having an inductive reactance of $j3\,\Omega$ per phase. Taking the phase voltage $V_{an}$ as reference, determine

## Guide

Transform the balanced delta to an equivalent $Y$ before combining parallel phase impedances. The $j3\,\Omega$ line impedance is in series with that combined phase load. Use the source phase voltage as the reference, compute source complex power, and distinguish source voltage from the reduced voltage at the load terminals. Assume positive $abc$ phase sequence when reporting line-voltage angles.

## Part a

The current, real power, and reactive power drawn from the supply.

### Solution

Use the formula sheet's [balanced delta-to-$Y$ relation](formula:delta-star), also shown in Lecture 3, PDF page 9, Ohm’s law for the per-phase circuit, and three-phase complex power from page 15:

$$
Z_{\Delta\to Y}=\frac{Z_\Delta}{3},\qquad I_a=\frac{V_{an}}{Z_{\mathrm{line}}+Z_{\mathrm{load}}},\qquad S_{\mathrm{supply}}=3V_{an}I_a^*.
$$

Given $R_\Delta=18\,\Omega$, $R_Y=12\,\Omega$, and $V_L=346.41\,\mathrm V$, first combine the two $Y$ phase resistances:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_{\Delta\to Y}&=18/3=6\,\Omega,\\
Z_{\mathrm{load}}&=12\parallel6=\frac{12(6)}{12+6}=4\,\Omega,\\
Z_{\mathrm{in}}&=4+j3=5\angle36.8699^\circ\,\Omega,\\
V_{an}&=\frac{346.41}{\sqrt3}\angle0^\circ=199.9999\angle0^\circ\,\mathrm V,\\
I_a&\approx\frac{200}{4+j3}=\frac{200(4-j3)}{4^2+3^2}=32-j24\,\mathrm A.
\end{aligned}
$$

The rounding of $346.41/\sqrt3$ to $200$ changes the results by less than one part per million. For a positive $abc$ sequence the other line currents are displaced by $-120^\circ$ and $+120^\circ$. Compute the power at the supply, including the line’s reactive demand:

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{\mathrm{supply}}&\approx3(200)(32+j24)=19200+j14400\,\mathrm{VA},\\
P_{\mathrm{loads}}&=3|I_a|^2(4)\approx19200\,\mathrm W,\\
Q_{\mathrm{line}}&=3|I_a|^2(3)\approx14400\,\mathrm{var}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The loads themselves are resistive, so all reactive power is absorbed by the line:

$$
\boxed{I_a\approx40\angle-36.8699^\circ\,\mathrm A,\quad I_b\approx40\angle-156.8699^\circ\,\mathrm A,\quad I_c\approx40\angle83.1301^\circ\,\mathrm A,\quad P\approx19.2\,\mathrm{kW},\quad Q\approx14.4\,\mathrm{kvar}.}
$$

### Source correction

The handwritten work in the scanned question PDF does not correctly form the parallel per-phase load. The official solution on PDF pages 12–13 correctly converts $18\,\Omega$ delta to $6\,\Omega$ $Y$ and then uses $12\parallel6=4\,\Omega$.

## Part b

The line-to-neutral and the line-to-line voltage of phase $a$ at the combined load terminals.

### Solution

Use the per-phase line-drop equation and positive-sequence line-voltage relation from Lecture 3, PDF pages 6 and 18:

$$
V_{an,\mathrm{load}}=V_{an,\mathrm{supply}}-Z_{\mathrm{line}}I_a,
\qquad V_{ab,\mathrm{load}}=\sqrt3V_{an,\mathrm{load}}\angle30^\circ.
$$

Using $I_a\approx32-j24\,\mathrm A$ from (a) and the source reference $V_{an,\mathrm{supply}}\approx200\angle0^\circ\,\mathrm V$, calculate the line drop and the load phase voltage:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_{\mathrm{line}}I_a&=j3(32-j24)=72+j96\,\mathrm V,\\
V_{an,\mathrm{load}}&=200-(72+j96)=128-j96\,\mathrm V,\\
|V_{an,\mathrm{load}}|&=\sqrt{128^2+96^2}=160\,\mathrm V,\\
\arg V_{an,\mathrm{load}}&=-\tan^{-1}(96/128)=-36.8699^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

Multiplying by $\sqrt3\angle30^\circ$ gives the phase-$a$ to phase-$b$ line voltage. Both angles remain referenced to the source phase voltage:

$$
\boxed{V_{an,\mathrm{load}}\approx160\angle-36.8699^\circ\,\mathrm V,\qquad V_{ab,\mathrm{load}}\approx277.128\angle-6.8699^\circ\,\mathrm V.}
$$

### Source correction

Official solution PDF page 13 prints the unit “$\mathrm A$” after $V_2=160\angle-36.87^\circ$; it is a voltage and its unit must be volts. The official line-voltage magnitude $277.1\,\mathrm V$ is consistent. The prompt does not explicitly specify phase sequence: the line-voltage magnitude is unchanged for either sequence, while the stated $+30^\circ$ shift assumes positive $abc$ sequence.
