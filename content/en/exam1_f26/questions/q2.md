## Question

Derive and show that single phase AC has a double frequency term in the power.

## Guide

Start from instantaneous power as the product of voltage and current. Represent both as sinusoids at the same angular frequency, retain their phase angles, and use the product-to-sum identity. Do not confuse peak amplitudes with RMS values.

## Part a

Derive and show that single phase AC has a double frequency term in the power.

### Solution

Use the instantaneous-power definition and sinusoidal forms in Lecture 2, PDF pages 6–9:

$$
p(t)=v(t)i(t),\qquad v(t)=V_m\cos(\omega t+\theta_v),\qquad i(t)=I_m\cos(\omega t+\theta_i).
$$

Here $V_m,I_m$ are peak amplitudes; the question gives no numerical values and asks us to expose the frequency of the power oscillation. Apply the formula sheet's [product-to-sum identity](formula:product-to-sum)

$$
\cos A\cos B=\frac12\bigl[\cos(A-B)+\cos(A+B)\bigr].
$$

Substitute $A=\omega t+\theta_v$ and $B=\omega t+\theta_i$ so that their difference is constant and their sum contains $2\omega t$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
p(t)&=V_mI_m\cos(\omega t+\theta_v)\cos(\omega t+\theta_i)\\
&=\frac{V_mI_m}{2}\left[\cos(\theta_v-\theta_i)+\cos(2\omega t+\theta_v+\theta_i)\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

With RMS values $V=V_m/\sqrt2$ and $I=I_m/\sqrt2$, and $\theta=\theta_v-\theta_i$, the constant term is average real power. The remaining term oscillates at twice the supply frequency:

$$
\boxed{p(t)=\underbrace{VI\cos\theta}_{P}+\underbrace{VI\cos(2\omega t+\theta_v+\theta_i)}_{\text{power ripple at }2\omega},\qquad f_p=2f.}
$$
