## Question

Three loads are connected in parallel across a $1400\,\mathrm V$ (rms), $60\mathrm{Hz}$, single phase supply as shown in figure below.

Load 1: Inductive load, $125\,\mathrm{kVA}$ and $0.28$ power factor.

Load 2: Capacitive load, $10\,\mathrm{kW}$ and $40\,\mathrm{kVAR}$.

Load 3: Resistive load of $15\,\mathrm{kW}$.

## Guide

Add the loads' complex powers, taking inductive reactive power as positive and capacitive reactive power as negative. For the shunt capacitor, keep real power fixed and calculate the reduction in reactive power needed for the target lagging power factor.

## Part a

Find the total $\mathrm{kW}$, $\mathrm{kVAR}$, $\mathrm{kVA}$ and the supply power factor.

### Solution

Use the rms complex-power relation and power-factor definition from [Lecture 2, pages 10](/pages/lecture-02/10.webp)–[14](/pages/lecture-02/14.webp):

$$
S=P+jQ=VI^*,\quad P=|S|\cos\phi,\quad Q=|S|\sin\phi,\quad |S|=\sqrt{P^2+Q^2},\quad \mathrm{PF}=\frac{P}{|S|}
$$

Load 1 is inductive, with $|S_1|=125\,\mathrm{kVA}$ and $\cos\phi_1=0.28$. Load 2 is capacitive, so its stated $40\,\mathrm{kvar}$ magnitude means $Q_2=-40\,\mathrm{kvar}$. The resistor has $Q_3=0$. Substitute:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_1&=125(0.28)=35\,\mathrm{kW},\\
Q_1&=125\sqrt{1-0.28^2}=125(0.96)=120\,\mathrm{kvar},\\
S_1&=(35+j120)\,\mathrm{kVA},\quad S_2=(10-j40)\,\mathrm{kVA},\quad S_3=(15+j0)\,\mathrm{kVA},\\
S_{\mathrm{tot}}&=(35+10+15)+j(120-40+0)=(60+j80)\,\mathrm{kVA},\\
|S_{\mathrm{tot}}|&=\sqrt{60^2+80^2}=100\,\mathrm{kVA},\qquad \phi=\tan^{-1}(80/60)=53.1301^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

Positive total reactive power means the supply current lags the voltage. Therefore

$$
\boxed{P=60\,\mathrm{kW},\quad Q=80\,\mathrm{kvar},\quad |S|=100\,\mathrm{kVA},\quad \mathrm{PF}=60/100=0.6\ \text{lagging}}
$$

## Part b

A capacitor of negligible resistance is connected in parallel with the above loads to improve the power factor to $0.8$ lagging. Determine the capacitance (micro Farad) and the $\mathrm{kVAR}$ rating of this capacitor.

### Solution

Use the power-factor correction relation in [Lecture 2, pages 18](/pages/lecture-02/18.webp)–[21](/pages/lecture-02/21.webp). For a lossless shunt capacitor, $I_C=j\omega CV$, so its complex power is $S_C=VI_C^*=-j\omega C|V|^2$:

$$
Q'=P\tan(\cos^{-1}\mathrm{PF}'),\qquad Q_C=Q'-Q=-\omega C|V|^2,\qquad \omega=2\pi f
$$

From part (a), $P=60\,\mathrm{kW}$ and $Q=80\,\mathrm{kvar}$. The capacitor sees the full $1400\,\mathrm V$ rms supply at $60\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Substitute the target lagging factor $0.8$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\phi'&=\cos^{-1}(0.8)=36.8699^\circ,\qquad \tan\phi'=\frac{\sqrt{1-0.8^2}}{0.8}=0.75,\\
Q'&=60(0.75)=45\,\mathrm{kvar},\\
Q_C&=45-80=-35\,\mathrm{kvar},\\
C&=\frac{-Q_C}{2\pi fV^2}=\frac{35000}{2\pi(60)(1400)^2}\,\mathrm F
=4.73675\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm F.
\end{aligned}
$$

The capacitor's nameplate rating is the positive magnitude of supplied reactive power. Converting farads to microfarads gives

$$
\boxed{C\approx47.37\,\mu\mathrm F,\qquad |Q_C|=35\,\mathrm{kvar}\ \text{capacitive}\ (Q_C=-35\,\mathrm{kvar})}
$$
