## Question

A 440-V, 3-phase voltage feeds a balanced delta-connected 3-phase load with an impedance of $(2+j15)\,\Omega$/phase.

## Guide

Each delta branch receives the line-to-line voltage. Divide by the branch impedance, then use Kirchhoff's current law at each terminal to obtain line currents. State a phase sequence and reference before assigning angles.

## Part a

Calculate the line current and represent in phasor form.

### Solution

Use the formula sheet's [delta-current relations](formula:delta), also shown in Lecture 3, page 8, with branch currents directed $a\to b$, $b\to c$ and $c\to a$ and line currents directed into the load:

$$
I_{ab}=\frac{V_{ab}}{Z_\Delta},\quad I_{bc}=\frac{V_{bc}}{Z_\Delta},\quad I_{ca}=\frac{V_{ca}}{Z_\Delta},\qquad
I_a=I_{ab}-I_{ca},\ I_b=I_{bc}-I_{ab},\ I_c=I_{ca}-I_{bc}
$$

Use rms phasors and positive sequence with $V_{ab}=440\angle0^\circ\,\mathrm V$, $V_{bc}=440\angle-120^\circ\,\mathrm V$ and $V_{ca}=440\angle120^\circ\,\mathrm V$. The question specifies no reference or sequence; this convention fixes the reported angles. Substitute $Z_\Delta=2+j15\,\Omega$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_\Delta&=\sqrt{2^2+15^2}\angle\tan^{-1}(15/2)=15.1327\angle82.4054^\circ\,\Omega,\\
I_{ab}&=\frac{440(2-j15)}{2^2+15^2}=3.8428-j28.8210=29.0760\angle-82.4054^\circ\,\mathrm A,\\
I_{bc}&=I_{ab}e^{-j120^\circ}=-26.8811+j11.0825\,\mathrm A,\\
I_{ca}&=I_{ab}e^{j120^\circ}=23.0383+j17.7384\,\mathrm A,\\
I_a&=(3.8428-j28.8210)-(23.0383+j17.7384)=-19.1955-j46.5594\,\mathrm A.
\end{aligned}
$$

The other two line currents follow by $120^\circ$ rotations. With $|I_L|=\sqrt3(29.0760)=50.3611\,\mathrm A$,

$$
\boxed{I_a=50.3611\angle-112.4054^\circ\,\mathrm A,\quad I_b=50.3611\angle127.5946^\circ\,\mathrm A,\quad I_c=50.3611\angle7.5946^\circ\,\mathrm A}
$$

### Source correction

PDF page 9 prints stray “16.34” and “$21.79^\circ$” values in two phase-current lines, inconsistent with $440/(2+j15)$; their rectangular values correspond to 29.0760 A and the $82.4054^\circ$ impedance angle. Its $a,b,c$ branch labels also obscure which current is subtracted at a terminal. The explicit $ab,bc,ca$ convention above yields the printed line-current magnitude and reference angle without mixing branch directions. A different stated sequence/reference changes angles, not magnitudes or power.

## Part b

Calculate the power absorbed per phase and

### Solution

Use rms complex power from Lecture 2, page 13, and Ohm's law:

$$
P_\phi=\operatorname{Re}(V_\phi I_\phi^*)=|I_\phi|^2R=\frac{|V_\phi|^2R}{R^2+X^2}
$$

Each delta branch has $V_\phi=440\,\mathrm V$, $R=2\,\Omega$ and $X=15\,\Omega$. Substitute the actual branch values:

$$
P_\phi=\frac{440^2(2)}{2^2+15^2}=\frac{387200}{229}\,\mathrm W.
$$

Therefore, for each of the three equal branches,

$$
P_\phi=\boxed{1690.83\,\mathrm W\approx1.691\,\mathrm{kW}}
$$

## Part c

Calculate the sum of all phase currents. Explain why this sum has such a value.

### Solution

Use the balanced-phasor relation from Lecture 3, pages 8 and 12:

$$
I_{ab}+I_{bc}+I_{ca}=I_{ab}(1+e^{-j120^\circ}+e^{j120^\circ})
$$

Substitute the branch currents obtained in part (a), all 29.0760 A in magnitude and $120^\circ$ apart:

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{ab}+I_{bc}+I_{ca}
&=(3.8428-j28.8210)+(-26.8811+j11.0825)+(23.0383+j17.7384)\,\mathrm A\\
&\approx(0+j0)\,\mathrm A,\\
1+e^{-j120^\circ}+e^{j120^\circ}&=1+2\cos120^\circ=1-1=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

The exact zero comes from equal magnitudes and symmetric phase spacing, not from adding scalar magnitudes. The line currents also sum to zero by telescoping their branch-current differences. Thus

$$
\boxed{I_{ab}+I_{bc}+I_{ca}=0\,\mathrm A,\qquad I_a+I_b+I_c=0\,\mathrm A}
$$
