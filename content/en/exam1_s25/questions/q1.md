## Question

Describe magnetic losses in a transformer.

## Guide

Separate the energy dissipated by cyclic magnetization from the Joule heating of currents induced inside the core. Use the hysteresis-loop area to explain the first mechanism and Faraday's law to explain the second.

## Part a

Describe magnetic losses in a transformer.

### Solution

Use Lecture 4, pages 15–16: the hysteresis-loop integral gives energy dissipated per cycle per unit core volume; induced eddy currents dissipate resistive power. For core volume $\mathcal V$ and frequency $f$,

$$
W_h=\mathcal V\oint H\,dB,\qquad P_h=fW_h,\qquad P_e=\int_{\mathcal V}\frac{|\mathbf J_e|^2}{\sigma}\,d\mathcal V
$$

The question asks for the mechanisms, rather than a numerical loss. Here $H$ is magnetic field strength, $B$ is flux density, $\sigma$ is conductivity and $\mathbf J_e$ is the induced current density. During each alternating cycle, irreversible domain-wall motion and domain rotation make magnetization lag the applied field. The energy represented by the enclosed $B$–$H$ loop becomes heat. This occurs even below saturation; a low-loss, narrow-loop core material reduces it.

The changing core flux induces closed electric fields by Faraday's law. In a conducting core these drive eddy-current loops, whose resistance produces heat. Thin insulated laminations interrupt large loops and increase their effective resistance. For sinusoidal excitation in the thin-lamination approximation, the specific loss scales as $p_e=k_e f^2B_{\max}^2$, with $k_e$ dependent on material resistivity and sheet thickness. Copper winding losses are separate from these magnetic core losses.

Combining the two magnetic loss mechanisms gives

$$
P_{\mathrm{core}}=\boxed{f\mathcal V\oint H\,dB+\int_{\mathcal V}\frac{|\mathbf J_e|^2}{\sigma}\,d\mathcal V=P_h+P_e}
$$

With sinusoidal excitation maintained, these core losses persist even when the secondary winding is open-circuited.

### Source correction

The official answer on PDF page 3 attributes hysteresis loss to magnetization saturation. Saturation is not required: a nonzero hysteresis-loop area causes loss during cyclic magnetization below saturation as well. Lecture 4, page 15 gives the loop-energy relation used here.
