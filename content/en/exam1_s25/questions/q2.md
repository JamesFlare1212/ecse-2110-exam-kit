## Question

Derive and show that single phase AC has a double frequency term in the power.

## Guide

Start from instantaneous power as the product of sinusoidal voltage and current. Apply the product-to-sum identity, keeping peak amplitudes distinct from rms phasor magnitudes.

## Part a

Derive and show that single phase AC has a double frequency term in the power.

### Solution

Use the instantaneous-power relation in [Lecture 2, pages 7](/pages/lecture-02/7.webp)–[9](/pages/lecture-02/9.webp), and the formula sheet's [product-to-sum identity](formula:product-to-sum):

$$
p(t)=v(t)i(t),\qquad \cos A\cos B=\tfrac12[\cos(A-B)+\cos(A+B)]
$$

For steady sinusoidal single-phase operation, let $V_m,I_m$ denote peak values and $\theta_v,\theta_i$ the phases. No numerical amplitudes are specified. Substitute the general waveforms to determine the frequencies present:

$$
\begin{aligned}
v(t)&=V_m\cos(\omega t+\theta_v),\qquad i(t)=I_m\cos(\omega t+\theta_i),\\
p(t)&=V_mI_m\cos(\omega t+\theta_v)\cos(\omega t+\theta_i)\\
&=\frac{V_mI_m}{2}\left[\cos\bigl((\omega t+\theta_v)-(\omega t+\theta_i)\bigr)+\cos\bigl((\omega t+\theta_v)+(\omega t+\theta_i)\bigr)\right]\\
&=\frac{V_mI_m}{2}[\cos(\theta_v-\theta_i)+\cos(2\omega t+\theta_v+\theta_i)].
\end{aligned}
$$

The first term is constant. The second has angular frequency $2\omega$, hence frequency $2f$ when the source frequency is $f=\omega/(2\pi)$. Its average over a source period $T=2\pi/\omega$ vanishes: the amplitude is constant with respect to $t$, so

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac1T\int_0^T\cos(2\omega t+\theta_v+\theta_i)\,dt
&=\frac1T\left[\frac{\sin(2\omega t+\theta_v+\theta_i)}{2\omega}\right]_0^T\\
&=\frac{\sin(4\pi+\theta_v+\theta_i)-\sin(\theta_v+\theta_i)}{2\omega T}=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

Write $V=V_m/\sqrt2$ and $I=I_m/\sqrt2$ for rms magnitudes. The result is

$$
\boxed{p(t)=VI\cos(\theta_v-\theta_i)+VI\cos(2\omega t+\theta_v+\theta_i),\qquad f_{\mathrm{ripple}}=2f}
$$
