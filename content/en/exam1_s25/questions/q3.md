## Question

In the figure below, the delta connected load is the equivalent of the star connected load. Derive the relationship between $Z_Y$ and $Z_\Delta$.

## Guide

All three impedances within each load are equal. Compare the impedances seen between terminals $a$ and $b$ with terminal $c$ open; equivalent three-terminal networks must give the same result. The isolated star point is an internal node, not an additional connected terminal.

## Part a

Derive the relationship between $Z_Y$ and $Z_\Delta$.

### Solution

Use series and parallel impedance combination, consistent with the formula sheet's [balanced delta–$Y$ relation](formula:delta-star), also shown in [Lecture 3, page 9](/pages/lecture-03/9.webp):

$$
Z_{\mathrm{series}}=Z_1+Z_2,\qquad Z_1\parallel Z_2=\frac{Z_1Z_2}{Z_1+Z_2}
$$

Given equal star branches $Z_Y$ and equal delta branches $Z_\Delta$, find their equivalence relation. Apply a test voltage between $a$ and $b$, leaving terminal $c$ externally open. In the star, no current leaves $c$, so its branch carries zero current; the other two branches are in series. In the delta, the direct $a$–$b$ branch is in parallel with the series path through $c$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_{ab,Y}&=Z_Y+Z_Y=2Z_Y,\\
Z_{ab,\Delta}&=Z_\Delta\parallel(2Z_\Delta)
=\frac{Z_\Delta(2Z_\Delta)}{Z_\Delta+2Z_\Delta}
=\frac{2Z_\Delta}{3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Equating the terminal impedances gives $2Z_Y=2Z_\Delta/3$. By symmetry the same equality holds for terminal pairs $b,c$ and $c,a$, establishing the balanced three-terminal equivalence. Equivalently, for balanced voltages, $I_a=(V_{ab}+V_{ac})/Z_\Delta=3V_{an}/Z_\Delta=V_{an}/Z_Y$.

Thus

$$
\boxed{Z_Y=\frac{Z_\Delta}{3}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad Z_\Delta=3Z_Y}
$$
