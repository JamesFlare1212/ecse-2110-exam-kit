## Question

A three-phase Y-connected synchronous generator is driven by a primer mover to produce $60\,\mathrm{Hz}$ AC voltage. It has a synchronous reactance of $j2\,\Omega$ per phase and negligible synchronous resistance. When the excitation current is $10\,\mathrm{A}$, and no load is connected to stator windings, the line-to-line voltage at the output is measured to be $100\,\mathrm{V}$. Now we increase the excitation current to $20\,\mathrm{A}$ and connect a three phase Y-connected load with an impedance of $10+j10\,\Omega$ per phase to the generator. ## Source correction

“Primer mover” is transcribed as printed. The printed $j2\,\Omega$ is the synchronous impedance with negligible resistance, so this solution uses $X_s=2\,\Omega$. Doubling field current does not, in general, exactly double flux in a saturated machine (Lecture 10, PDF page 15). No magnetization curve is supplied; the numerical result below therefore assumes an unsaturated linear magnetic circuit and unchanged $X_s$. Without that assumption the loaded voltage is underdetermined.

## Part a

Draw the single-phase equivalent circuit of the system and label the impedances.

### Solution

Use the generator relation from Lecture 10, PDF page 18, with current directed from generator to load:

$$
\underline E_A=\underline V_\phi+jX_s\underline I_A,\qquad
\underline V_\phi=Z_L\underline I_A
$$

The diagram places the internal phase source in series with the synchronous reactance and the phase load, with a common neutral return. Both generator and load are Y-connected, so line current equals phase current. Insert the printed impedances:

$$
\underline E_A=\bigl[j2+(10+j10)\bigr]\underline I_A,\qquad
\boxed{Z_s=j2\,\Omega,\quad Z_L=10+j10\,\Omega,\quad Z_{\mathrm{series}}=10+j12\,\Omega.}
$$

## Part b

Find the RMS value of the line-to-line voltage at the load side.

### Solution

Use the formula sheet's [generated-voltage relation](formula:generated-voltage), also shown in Lecture 10, PDF page 14, the Y-connection voltage relation, and the generator circuit from part (a):

$$
|E_A|\propto f\Phi,\qquad V_{LL}=\sqrt3|V_\phi|,\qquad
\underline V_\phi=\underline E_A\frac{Z_L}{Z_L+jX_s}
$$

At no load, current is zero, so $|E_{A0}|=100/\sqrt3\,\mathrm{V}$. Speed and frequency remain fixed. With the stated linear-flux assumption, scaling field current from $10$ to $20\,\mathrm{A}$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
|E_{A1}|&=\frac{20}{10}\frac{100}{\sqrt3}=115.470\,\mathrm{V},\\
\underline V_\phi&=\frac{200}{\sqrt3}\frac{10+j10}{10+j12}\,\mathrm{V},\\
|V_\phi|&=115.470\frac{\sqrt{10^2+10^2}}{\sqrt{10^2+12^2}}=104.5417\,\mathrm{V}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Convert the loaded phase magnitude to the requested line-to-line RMS value:

$$
V_{LL}=\sqrt3(104.5417)=200\sqrt{\frac{200}{244}}=\boxed{181.0715\,\mathrm{V}.}
$$
