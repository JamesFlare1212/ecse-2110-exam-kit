## Question

A three-phase $1.732\text{-}\mathrm{kV}$ $2$-pole $60\text{-}\mathrm{Hz}$ $Y$-connected synchronous generator has a synchronous reactance of $j1\,\Omega$ per phase and negligible armature resistance. The synchronous generator is driven by a turbine.

Suppose under certain load condition, the RMS value of the line-to-line terminal voltage of the generator is $1.732\,\mathrm{kV}$, and the generator supplies $1.5\,\mathrm{MW}$ real power to the load. The power angle is $30^\circ$ at this operating condition.

## Guide

First obtain mechanical angular speed from frequency and pole count. With losses neglected, shaft power equals delivered real power. Convert line voltage to the $Y$ phase voltage before using the power-angle relation; then recover the load current as a phasor to determine whether the power factor leads or lags.

## Source correction

The printed $j1\,\Omega$ denotes synchronous impedance; $X_s=1\,\Omega$ is its real reactance parameter. Calculations retain the printed $1732\,\mathrm{V}$ instead of silently replacing it with $1000\sqrt3\,\mathrm{V}$. The difference affects only the last few reported digits.

## Part a

How much torque is supplied by the turbine to the generator, assuming that all losses are neglected?

### Solution

Use the formula sheet's [synchronous-speed relation](formula:synchronous-speed), also shown in Lecture 10, PDF page 9, and the mechanical power definition $P_{\mathrm{mech}}=T\omega_m$:

$$
n_s=\frac{120f}{p},\qquad\omega_m=\frac{2\pi n_s}{60},\qquad T=\frac{P_{\mathrm{mech}}}{\omega_m}
$$

Given two poles and $60\,\mathrm{Hz}$, the shaft turns at $3600\,\mathrm{rpm}$. Neglecting all losses, $P_{\mathrm{mech}}=1.5\times10^6\,\mathrm{W}$. Substitute to obtain

$$
\omega_m=\frac{2\pi}{60}\frac{120(60)}2=120\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s},\qquad
T=\frac{1.5\times10^6}{120\pi}=\boxed{3978.87\,\mathrm{N\,m}.}
$$

## Part b

What is the RMS value of the internal generated voltage in each phase of the generator?

### Solution

Use the formula sheet's [generator power-angle relation](formula:power-angle), also shown for the round-rotor model in Lecture 10, PDF page 24:

$$
P=\frac{3|E_A|V_\phi}{X_s}\sin\delta,\qquad V_\phi=\frac{V_{LL}}{\sqrt3}
$$

Insert the printed $P=1.5\,\mathrm{MW}$, $X_s=1\,\Omega$, $V_{LL}=1732\,\mathrm{V}$ and $\delta=30^\circ$:

$$
V_\phi=\frac{1732}{\sqrt3}=999.9707\,\mathrm{V},\qquad
|E_A|=\frac{(1.5\times10^6)(1)}{3(1732/\sqrt3)\sin30^\circ}
=\boxed{1000.0293\,\mathrm{V}\ \text{per phase}.}
$$

## Part c

What is the power factor of the load?

### Solution

Use the generator circuit relation from Lecture 10, PDF page 18, and the complex-power relation on PDF page 20. Current is positive from generator to load, with terminal voltage as phase reference:

$$
\underline I_A=\frac{|E_A|\angle\delta-V_\phi}{jX_s},\qquad
S_{\mathrm{load}}=3V_\phi\underline I_A^*,\qquad\mathrm{pf}=\frac{\Re S_{\mathrm{load}}}{|S_{\mathrm{load}}|}
$$

Use $|E_A|=1000.0293\,\mathrm{V}$ from part (b), retaining unrounded values in the calculation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\underline I_A&=\frac{1000.0293(\cos30^\circ+j\sin30^\circ)-999.9707}{j(1)}\\
&=500.0147+j133.9199\,\mathrm{A},\\
\angle I_A&=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{133.9199}{500.0147}\right)=14.9937^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

The load current leads its terminal voltage, so the load receives negative reactive power. Its power factor is

$$
\mathrm{pf}=\frac{500.0147}{\sqrt{500.0147^2+133.9199^2}}=\boxed{0.965954\ \text{leading}.}
$$
