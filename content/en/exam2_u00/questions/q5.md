## Question

A $600\,\mathrm{V}$ three-phase $\Delta$-connected synchronous motor is connected to a $600\,\mathrm{V}$ bus. The motor has a synchronous reactance of $j4\,\Omega$ per phase and negligible armature.

## Guide

In a delta connection, winding voltage equals line voltage and line-current magnitude is $\sqrt3$ times winding current. Use current into the motor and internal voltage lagging the terminal voltage. Determine internal voltage from developed power, then form the current phasor. For the changed operating condition, maximize the sine in the power-angle equation and recalculate reactive power from the corresponding current.

## Source correction

The printed phrase “negligible armature” is incomplete; this solution assumes negligible armature resistance. The printed $j4\,\Omega$ is interpreted as phase impedance, so $X_s=4\,\Omega$. In part (b), “adjust the prime mover” is retained, although a motor normally drives a mechanical load; the intended adjustment is taken to set shaft loading at the pull-out power. Exact maximum developed power is the ideal steady-state stability boundary, not a robust operating point. Scaling internal voltage by the field-current increase assumes a linear magnetic circuit; no saturation curve is supplied.

## Part a

Under certain load condition, the internal voltage lags the terminal voltage by $60^\circ$ in each phase, and the developed power in the motor is $40\,\mathrm{kW}$. Find the RMS value of the line current.

### Solution

Reverse the formula sheet's [generator current](formula:generator-current) and [power-angle](formula:power-angle) conventions, also used in Lecture 10, PDF pages 18 and 24, to obtain the motor relations, using positive $\delta$ for the lag of internal voltage:

$$
P_{\mathrm{dev}}=\frac{3V_\phi|E_A|}{X_s}\sin\delta,\qquad
\underline I_\phi=\frac{V_\phi-|E_A|\angle(-\delta)}{jX_s},\qquad
I_L=\sqrt3|I_\phi|
$$

The delta winding voltage is $V_\phi=600\,\mathrm{V}$. Substitute the given $40\,\mathrm{kW}$ and $60^\circ$ to determine the internal voltage and phase current:

$$
\begin{aligned}
|E_{A,a}|&=\frac{40000(4)}{3(600)\sin60^\circ}=102.64005\,\mathrm{V},\\
\underline I_{\phi,a}&=\frac{600-102.64005(0.5-j0.8660254)}{j4}\\
&=22.22222-j137.16999\,\mathrm{A},\\
|I_{\phi,a}|&=\sqrt{22.22222^2+137.16999^2}=138.95839\,\mathrm{A}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Convert winding current to line-current magnitude:

$$
I_{L,a}=\sqrt3(138.95839)=\boxed{240.6830\,\mathrm{A}\ \text{RMS}.}
$$

## Part b

We increase the field current by $20\%$ and adjust the prime mover such that the motor achieves it maximum developed power. Find the new torque angle and the RMS value of the new line current.

### Solution

Use the motor power-angle relation derived in part (a). At fixed voltage and excitation its positive maximum occurs when $\sin\delta=1$:

$$
P_{\mathrm{dev}}=\frac{3V_\phi|E_A|}{X_s}\sin\delta,\qquad
\frac{dP_{\mathrm{dev}}}{d\delta}=\frac{3V_\phi|E_A|}{X_s}\cos\delta
$$

Setting the derivative to zero gives $\delta_b=90^\circ$; the second derivative is negative there. With the linear-excitation assumption, increase $|E_{A,a}|=102.64005\,\mathrm{V}$ from part (a) by the specified fraction:

$$
\begin{aligned}
|E_{A,b}|&=1.2(102.64005)=123.16806\,\mathrm{V},\\
\underline I_{\phi,b}&=\frac{600-123.16806\angle(-90^\circ)}{j4}\\
&=\frac{600+j123.16806}{j4}=30.79201-j150\,\mathrm{A},\\
|I_{\phi,b}|&=\sqrt{30.79201^2+150^2}=153.12788\,\mathrm{A}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The torque angle is stated as a positive lag; the signed angle of $E_A$ relative to $V_\phi$ is negative. The requested new values are

$$
\boxed{\delta_b=90^\circ\ \text{lag}\ (\angle E_{A,b}=-90^\circ),\qquad
I_{L,b}=\sqrt3(153.12788)=265.2253\,\mathrm{A}\ \text{RMS}.}
$$

## Part c

What is the reactive power the motor consumes? What is the RMS value of the current in each phase of its stator windings?

### Solution

Use the complex-power relation from Lecture 2, PDF page 13 (also used in Lecture 10, PDF page 20), with current into the motor and the delta phase voltage:

$$
S_{\mathrm{in}}=3\underline V_\phi\underline I_\phi^*,\qquad
Q_{\mathrm{in}}=\Im S_{\mathrm{in}},\qquad I_\phi=\frac{I_L}{\sqrt3}
$$

Interpret this part as continuing the new maximum-power condition in part (b). With $V_\phi=600\angle0^\circ\,\mathrm{V}$ and $\underline I_{\phi,b}=30.79201-j150\,\mathrm{A}$, substitute into complex power:

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{\mathrm{in},b}&=3(600)(30.79201+j150)\\
&=55425.63+j270000\,\mathrm{VA},\\
I_{\phi,b}&=\frac{265.2253}{\sqrt3}=153.12788\,\mathrm{A}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Positive reactive power denotes absorption by the motor. Thus, at the condition established in part (b),

$$
\boxed{Q_{\mathrm{in},b}=270.000\,\mathrm{kvar},\qquad I_{\phi,b}=153.1279\,\mathrm{A}\ \text{RMS}.}
$$

### Source correction

Part (c) does not explicitly say whether it refers to (a) or (b). Sequential reading gives the new condition in (b), used above. If the original condition in (a) was intended, direct reuse of its current gives $Q_{\mathrm{in},a}=3(600)(137.16999)=246.906\,\mathrm{kvar}$ and winding-current magnitude $138.9584\,\mathrm{A}$. These are different operating conditions, not interchangeable answers.
