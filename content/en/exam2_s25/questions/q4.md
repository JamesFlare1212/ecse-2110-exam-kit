## Question

Explain about the loss of synchronization in synchronous machines.

## Guide

A synchronous state requires a fixed relative angle between the rotor and rotating stator field. Use the power–angle curve to explain how load and disturbances can break this condition, and distinguish the static pull-out limit from transient stability.

## Part a

Explain about the loss of synchronization in synchronous machines.

### Solution

Use the formula sheet's [power–angle relation](formula:power-angle), also shown for the round-rotor model in Lecture 10, page 24, and mechanical power equal to torque times synchronous mechanical angular speed:

$$
P_e=\frac{3EV}{X_s}\sin\delta,\qquad T_e=\frac{P_e}{\omega_s}=T_{\max}\sin\delta
$$

Here $E$ and $V$ are phase RMS voltage magnitudes, $X_s$ is synchronous reactance per phase, and $\delta$ is electrical load angle. For a motor, take $\delta$ as the angle by which the stator field leads the rotor. At fixed voltage and excitation, increasing load torque requires a larger steady angle. An equilibrium and its synchronizing slope satisfy

$$
T_L=T_{\max}\sin\delta_0,\qquad
\frac{dT_e}{d\delta}=T_{\max}\cos\delta
$$

On the normal branch $0<\delta_0<\pi/2$, a small increase in angle raises electromagnetic torque and tends to restore equilibrium. At $\delta=\pi/2$, the torque reaches its maximum and the restoring slope vanishes. If $T_L>T_{\max}$, no steady angle can supply the required torque. The motor slows relative to the rotating field, the angle keeps advancing, and poles slip instead of remaining locked. A generator likewise loses a fixed rotor–field angle when mechanical input or disturbances exceed its stability capability.

A sudden load change, voltage sag, excitation loss or fault can also produce an unstable angle swing even when a final static equilibrium would exist. Sustained loss of locking produces speed and torque oscillations and large currents, and protection normally disconnects the machine. In the stated simple static model,

$$
\boxed{T_L>T_{\max}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{no synchronous equilibrium};\qquad T_{\mathrm{pull\!-!out}}=T_{\max}\ \text{at}\ \delta=90^\circ}
$$
