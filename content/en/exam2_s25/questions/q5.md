## Question

Two dc machines of the following rating are required:

DC machine 1: $120\,\mathrm V$, $1500\,\mathrm{rpm}$, four poles

DC machine 2: $240\,\mathrm V$, $1500\,\mathrm{rpm}$, four poles

Coils are available that are rated at $4$ volts and $5$ amperes. For the same number of coils to be used for both machines, determine the

## Guide

Use the simplex lap and wave parallel-path counts, then relate terminal voltage to series coils per path. Compare total coil counts and sum the path currents. These are electrical path-count ratings; a physical wave-winding layout has an additional closure constraint.

## Source correction

The cover date is July 15, 2025, while the footers say “Summer 2023”; `e2-s25` follows the cover with the conflict explicitly retained. The handwritten answer on PDF page 11 uses the elementary simplex path model, neglecting internal voltage drops and losses. A conventional closed simplex wave winding also needs an integer commutator pitch $y_c=(C\pm1)/(P/2)$. For $C=120$ and $P=4$, this is $59.5$ or $60.5$, so the intended $120$-active-coil result alone is not a realizable conventional closed wave layout. A changed coil/layout specification or a suitable dummy-coil design would need further engineering; a dummy coil is inactive and cannot silently be counted as a $4\,\mathrm V$ active coil. The answers below reproduce the intended electrical sizing with this limitation stated, rather than claiming a complete winding design.

## Part a

Type of armature winding for each machine.

### Solution

Use the simplex winding relations illustrated in Lecture 9, pages 5–6, together with voltage addition along each parallel path:

$$
a_{\mathrm{lap}}=P,\qquad a_{\mathrm{wave}}=2,\qquad V=\frac{C}{a}E_c
$$

Given $P=4$, coil voltage $E_c=4\,\mathrm V$, and the same total active-coil count $C$ for the two voltages, find the winding type for each machine. Divide the machine voltage relations to eliminate $C$ and $E_c$:

$$
\frac{V_2}{V_1}=\frac{a_1}{a_2}=\frac{240}{120}=2,\qquad
\frac{a_{\mathrm{lap}}}{a_{\mathrm{wave}}}=\frac{4}{2}=2
$$

Thus, within the intended simplex electrical path model,

$$
\boxed{120\,\mathrm V:\ \text{simplex lap},\ a_1=4;\qquad 240\,\mathrm V:\ \text{simplex wave},\ a_2=2}
$$

## Part b

Number of coils required for each machine.

### Solution

Use the series-voltage and total-coil relations; the parallel path counts come from part (a), not from additional given data:

$$
C_{\mathrm{path}}=\frac{V}{E_c},\qquad C=aC_{\mathrm{path}}
$$

Given $E_c=4\,\mathrm V$ per active coil, compute the number in series per path and then the total:

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{\mathrm{path},1}&=\frac{120}{4}=30,&C_1&=4(30),\\
C_{\mathrm{path},2}&=\frac{240}{4}=60,&C_2&=2(60).
\end{aligned}
$$

The intended active-coil count in the electrical model is

$$
\boxed{C_1=C_2=120\ \text{active coils, with 30 or 60 in series per path, respectively}}
$$

## Part c

$\mathrm{kW}$ rating of each machine.

### Solution

Use current addition across parallel armature paths and the DC electrical power relation:

$$
I_a=aI_c,\qquad P_{\mathrm{elec}}=VI_a
$$

Each series path carries the coil's rated $I_c=5\,\mathrm A$. Using the path counts from part (a), calculate the total currents and electrical powers:

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{a1}&=4(5)=20\,\mathrm A,&P_1&=120(20)=2400\,\mathrm W,\\
I_{a2}&=2(5)=10\,\mathrm A,&P_2&=240(10)=2400\,\mathrm W.
\end{aligned}
$$

These are ideal electrical ratings at the specified coil voltage and current. Mechanical shaft output would require loss data, which are not supplied. In the question's electrical sizing model,

$$
\boxed{P_1=P_2=2.4\,\mathrm{kW}}
$$
