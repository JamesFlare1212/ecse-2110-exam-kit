## Question

What is the role of a commutator in a DC machine?

## Guide

Follow one rotating coil through a half turn. Its induced voltage reverses relative to its own terminals; the commutator changes which terminal contacts each stationary brush. Consider both generator output polarity and motor torque direction.

## Part a

What is the role of a commutator in a DC machine?

### Solution

Use Faraday's law and the electromagnetic torque relation underlying the DC generator and motor drawings in [Lecture 8, pages 8](/pages/lecture-08/8.webp)–[9](/pages/lecture-08/9.webp). For an illustrative single coil rotating in a sinusoidal field,

$$
e_{\mathrm{coil}}=E_m\sin\theta,\qquad T\propto\Phi i_{\mathrm{active}}
$$

The question asks for the commutator's role in a DC machine. In generator operation, the coil voltage changes sign after half a revolution. At the same time, the split conducting segments exchange the coil connections to the stationary brushes. Taking the brush polarity as positive, this ideal two-segment example gives

$$
v_{\mathrm{brush}}=\begin{cases}
e_{\mathrm{coil}},&0<\theta<\pi,\\
-e_{\mathrm{coil}},&\pi<\theta<2\pi.
\end{cases}
$$

Thus the external voltage is unidirectional but pulsating; multiple coils and segments reduce the ripple. In motor operation, the same mechanical switching reverses a coil's current as it moves between magnetic poles, maintaining a consistent electromagnetic torque direction. The brushes briefly bridge adjacent segments while the coil current reverses near the neutral plane; real machines require suitable brush placement and commutation to limit sparking.

The ideal single-coil generator result and the motor consequence are

$$
\boxed{v_{\mathrm{brush}}=|E_m\sin\theta|\ge0,\qquad T_{\mathrm{motor}}\ \text{retains its driving direction}}
$$
