## Question

A $1\phi$, $100\,\mathrm{kVA}$, $1000/100\,\mathrm V$ transformer gave the following test results:

open-circuit test (HV side open)

$100\,\mathrm V$, $6.0\,\mathrm A$, $400\,\mathrm W$

short-circuit test

$50\,\mathrm V$, $100\,\mathrm A$, $1800\,\mathrm W$

## Guide

Determine single-phase rated currents first. The open-circuit measurements belong to the energized LV winding; the short-circuit current identifies the HV-fed test interpretation. Refer the shunt branch by the square of the turns ratio, and use a leading current phasor when calculating regulation.

The printed problem does not explicitly name the energized side of the short-circuit test. Following the handwritten solution on PDF page 14, this solution interprets $100\,\mathrm A$ as the rated HV current, with the LV side shorted. The open-circuit test is explicitly LV-fed. The approximate test model neglects no-load copper loss and short-circuit core loss. The source provides no answer to part (c); the calculation below completes it and states its approximation.

## Part a

Determine the rated voltage and rated current for the high-voltage and low-voltage sides.

### Solution

Use the single-phase apparent-power rating relation; there is no three-phase $\sqrt3$ factor:

$$
S_N=V_N I_N,\qquad I_N=\frac{S_N}{V_N}
$$

Given $S_N=100\times10^3\,\mathrm{VA}$, $V_{HN}=1000\,\mathrm V$ and $V_{LN}=100\,\mathrm V$, substitute each side's rated voltage:

$$
I_{HN}=\frac{100\times10^3}{1000}=100\,\mathrm A,\qquad
I_{LN}=\frac{100\times10^3}{100}=1000\,\mathrm A
$$

Therefore the two rated voltage–current pairs are

$$
\boxed{(V_{HN},I_{HN})=(1000\,\mathrm V,100\,\mathrm A),\qquad (V_{LN},I_{LN})=(100\,\mathrm V,1000\,\mathrm A)}
$$

## Part b

Derive an approximate equivalent circuit referred to the HV side.

### Solution

Use the transformer test relations in Lecture 6, page 8, and the impedance-referral rule in its equivalent-circuit treatment:

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_c&=\frac{V_{oc}^2}{P_{oc}},&I_c&=\frac{P_{oc}}{V_{oc}},&I_m&=\sqrt{I_{oc}^2-I_c^2},&X_m&=\frac{V_{oc}}{I_m},\\
R_{eq}&=\frac{P_{sc}}{I_{sc}^2},&|Z_{eq}|&=\frac{V_{sc}}{I_{sc}},&X_{eq}&=\sqrt{|Z_{eq}|^2-R_{eq}^2},&Z_H&=a^2 Z_L.
\end{aligned}
$$

The open-circuit test energizes the LV side at $100\,\mathrm V$ with $6.0\,\mathrm A$ and $400\,\mathrm W$. Neglect no-load winding loss and separate the in-phase core-loss current from the magnetizing current:

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{cL}&=\frac{100^2}{400}=25\,\Omega,&I_{cL}&=\frac{400}{100}=4\,\mathrm A,\\
I_{mL}&=\sqrt{6^2-4^2}=\sqrt{20}=4.4721\,\mathrm A,&X_{mL}&=\frac{100}{\sqrt{20}}=22.3607\,\Omega.
\end{aligned}
$$

The turns ratio is $a=V_{HN}/V_{LN}=1000/100=10$. Multiply each LV shunt impedance by $a^2=100$ to refer it to HV:

$$
R_{cH}=100(25)=2500\,\Omega,\qquad X_{mH}=100(22.3607)=2236.07\,\Omega
$$

The interpreted HV-fed short-circuit test already gives HV-referred series parameters. Neglect core loss at the reduced test voltage and substitute the $50\,\mathrm V$, $100\,\mathrm A$ and $1800\,\mathrm W$ measurements:

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{eq,H}&=\frac{1800}{100^2}=0.18\,\Omega,\\
|Z_{eq,H}|&=\frac{50}{100}=0.5\,\Omega,\\
X_{eq,H}&=\sqrt{0.5^2-0.18^2}=\sqrt{0.2176}=0.466476\,\Omega.
\end{aligned}
$$

Place $R_{cH}$ and $jX_{mH}$ in parallel across the HV input, and $R_{eq,H}+jX_{eq,H}$ in series with the referred LV load, as in the revealed circuit. The parameters are

![HV-referred input-shunt equivalent circuit](figure:e2-s25-q6-equivalent)

$$
\boxed{R_{cH}=2.500\,\mathrm{k}\Omega,\quad X_{mH}=2.236\,\mathrm{k}\Omega,\quad R_{eq,H}=0.180\,\Omega,\quad X_{eq,H}=0.46648\,\Omega}
$$

### Source correction

The source rounds $I_{mL}$ to $4.47\,\mathrm A$ before evaluating $100/I_{mL}$, giving $X_{mL}=22.37\,\Omega$ and $X_{mH}=2237\,\Omega$. Retaining $\sqrt{20}$ until the end gives $22.3607$ and $2236.07$ ohms; this is a rounding difference, not a changed test datum. The source does not separate the two windings' individual resistance or leakage reactance; the tests identify their referred sums only.

## Part c

Determine the voltage regulation at full load, $0.6$ PF leading.

### Solution

Use the voltage-regulation definition in Lecture 6, page 10, and derive its usual first-order form from the approximate circuit of part (b). With the referred output voltage as the real reference and a leading current,

$$
\underline E_H=V_L'+\underline I_L'(R_{eq,H}+jX_{eq,H}),\qquad
\mathrm{VR}=\frac{|\underline E_H|-V_L'}{V_L'}\,100\%
$$

At rated output, $V_L'=1000\,\mathrm V$, $I_L'=100\,\mathrm A$ and $\cos\phi=0.6$, so $\sin\phi=\sqrt{1-0.6^2}=0.8$. The current is $100(0.6+j0.8)\,\mathrm A$. Multiply it by the series impedance from part (b):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta\underline V&=100(0.6+j0.8)(0.18+j0.466476)\\
&=100\bigl[(0.18)(0.6)-(0.466476)(0.8)\bigr]\\
&\quad+j100\bigl[(0.466476)(0.6)+(0.18)(0.8)\bigr]\\
&=-26.5181+j42.3886\,\mathrm V.
\end{aligned}
$$

The first-order voltage-change approximation keeps the component parallel to the output voltage; the reactance term has a minus sign for a leading load. Keeping the full magnitude within the same approximate circuit instead gives $|\underline E_H|=|973.4819+j42.3886|=974.4043\,\mathrm V$. Both methods predict a voltage rise under load, hence negative regulation:

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{VR}_{\mathrm{first\ order}}&\approx\frac{-26.5181}{1000}\,100\%=-2.652\%,\\
\mathrm{VR}_{\mathrm{phasor\ magnitude}}&=\frac{974.4043-1000}{1000}\,100\%=-2.560\%.
\end{aligned}}
$$
