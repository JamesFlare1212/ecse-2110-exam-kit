## Question

A $1\phi$, $5\,\mathrm{kVA}$, $240/120\,\mathrm V$, $60\,\mathrm{Hz}$ transformer has a core loss of $100\,\mathrm W$ at rated voltage and copper loss of $120\,\mathrm W$ at rated current. Calculate the efficiency for the following load condition:

## Guide

Convert apparent output power to real output using power factor. At constant rated voltage and frequency, treat the core loss as constant and scale copper loss with the square of the load-current fraction.

## Part a

It is delivering $5\,\mathrm{kVA}$ at rated voltage and a power factor of $0.8$.

### Solution

Use the transformer efficiency relation in Lecture 6, page 9; it is not on the Exam 2 reference sheet. At rated voltage and frequency, define the load fraction $x=S_{out}/S_N$ and use copper loss proportional to current squared:

$$
P_{out}=S_{out}\mathrm{PF},\qquad P_{cu}=x^2 P_{cu,N},\qquad
\eta=\frac{P_{out}}{P_{out}+P_{core}+P_{cu}}
$$

Given $S_{out}=5\,\mathrm{kVA}$, $\mathrm{PF}=0.8$, $P_{core}=100\,\mathrm W$ and $P_{cu,N}=120\,\mathrm W$, the load fraction is $x=5/5=1$. Substitute to find real output and losses:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{out}&=5000(0.8)=4000\,\mathrm W,\\
P_{cu}&=1^2(120)=120\,\mathrm W,\\
P_{in}&=4000+100+120=4220\,\mathrm W.
\end{aligned}
$$

The efficiency is

$$
\eta=\frac{4000}{4220}\,100\%=\boxed{94.79\%}
$$

## Part b

It is delivering $2\,\mathrm{kVA}$ at rated voltage and a power factor of $0.8$.

### Solution

Use the same transformer loss model from Lecture 6, page 9. At the stated constant voltage, the current fraction equals the apparent-power fraction:

$$
x=\frac{I}{I_N}=\frac{S_{out}}{S_N},\qquad
P_{cu}=x^2P_{cu,N},\qquad
\eta=\frac{S_{out}\mathrm{PF}}{S_{out}\mathrm{PF}+P_{core}+x^2P_{cu,N}}
$$

Given $S_{out}=2\,\mathrm{kVA}$, $S_N=5\,\mathrm{kVA}$ and $\mathrm{PF}=0.8$, the load fraction is $x=2/5=0.4$. Core loss remains $100\,\mathrm W$ at the rated voltage and $60\,\mathrm{Hz}$; it does not scale with output load. Compute

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{out}&=2000(0.8)=1600\,\mathrm W,\\
P_{cu}&=\left(\frac25\right)^2(120)=0.16(120)=19.2\,\mathrm W,\\
P_{in}&=1600+100+19.2=1719.2\,\mathrm W.
\end{aligned}
$$

The efficiency at this load is

$$
\eta=\frac{1600}{1719.2}\,100\%=\boxed{93.07\%}
$$
