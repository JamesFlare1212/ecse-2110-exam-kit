## Question

The speed of a $110\,\mathrm V$, $5\,\mathrm{hp}$ dc motor is controlled by a $1ϕ$ ac/dc full converter (i.e., controlled rectifier). The ac supply is $120\,\mathrm V$, $60\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Consider the dc motor and converter to be ideal and lossless. Assume a very large inductance in series with the motor. The motor voltage constant is $0.055\,\mathrm{V/rpm}$.

## Guide

Find rated current using the rated motor voltage, then keep that current at the requested reduced speed. For a lossless motor the required converter average voltage equals back emf. The true supply power factor includes current distortion and is smaller than the firing-angle cosine.

## Part a

Draw the power circuit.

### Solution

Use the four-SCR bridge topology shown in Lecture 11–12, PDF page 23, with the stated large series inductance and ideal DC motor. The bridge connects the AC source across its two leg midpoints; the motor and inductor connect in series between the upper positive and lower negative DC buses. Opposite pairs are fired alternately:

$$
(S_1,S_2)\ \text{at }\theta=\alpha+2k\pi,\qquad
(S_3,S_4)\ \text{at }\theta=\pi+\alpha+2k\pi.
$$

![Four-SCR bridge and ideal motor armature](figure:p2-f26-q5-circuit)

The revealed circuit uses that label ordering and retains the motor emf polarity positive at its upper terminal. With zero motor resistance, average inductor voltage vanishes, giving the circuit constraints

$$
\boxed{v_0=L\frac{di_0}{dt}+E_a,\quad \langle v_0\rangle=E_a,\quad i_0\simeq I_0>0}.
$$

## Part b(i)

For a speed of $1000\,\mathrm{rpm}$ and rated motor current,

Determine the firing angle of the converter.

### Solution

Use the [full-converter average voltage](formula:rectifier-full). The supplied motor constant is explicitly in volts per $\mathrm{rpm}$, so its back emf is $E_a=k_nn$:

$$
V_0=\frac{2\sqrt2V_s}{\pi}\cos\alpha=E_a.
$$

At $n=1000\,\mathrm{rpm}$, $E_a=0.055(1000)=55\,\mathrm V$. The lossless motor's rated current follows from its $5\,\mathrm{hp}$ rating at $110\,\mathrm V$:

$$
I_N=\frac{5(746)}{110}=33.909091\,\mathrm A,\qquad
\cos\alpha=\frac{55\pi}{2\sqrt2(120)}=0.509080337.
$$

Invert the cosine to obtain

$$
\alpha=\cos^{-1}(0.509080337)=\boxed{59.397409^\circ}.
$$

### Source correction

The source rounds this to $60^\circ$ after replacing $0.50908$ by $0.5$. The more accurate result is retained. At this speed and rated current, converted power is $55(33.909091)\,\mathrm W$, half the rated $5\,\mathrm{hp}$; the question requests rated current, not rated output power at reduced speed.

## Part b(ii)

For a speed of $1000\,\mathrm{rpm}$ and rated motor current,

Determine the rms value of the supply current and thyristor current.

### Solution

Use the RMS definition. The constant DC current flows through the source for the whole cycle and through each individual thyristor for half a cycle:

$$
I_{s,\mathrm{rms}}^2=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}I_0^2\,d\theta,\qquad
I_{T,\mathrm{rms}}^2=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_\alpha^{\pi+\alpha}I_0^2\,d\theta.
$$

Part b(i) gives $I_0=5(746)/110=33.909091\,\mathrm A$. Take its square outside each integral and evaluate the interval lengths:

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{s,\mathrm{rms}}^2&=\frac{I_0^2}{2\pi}[\theta]_0^{2\pi}=I_0^2\frac{2\pi-0}{2\pi}=I_0^2,\\
I_{T,\mathrm{rms}}^2&=\frac{I_0^2}{2\pi}[\theta]_\alpha^{\pi+\alpha}=I_0^2\frac{(\pi+\alpha)-\alpha}{2\pi}=\frac{I_0^2}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Taking positive square roots gives

$$
\boxed{I_{s,\mathrm{rms}}=33.909091\,\mathrm A,\qquad I_{T,\mathrm{rms}}=\frac{33.909091}{\sqrt2}=23.977348\,\mathrm A\ \text{per SCR}}.
$$

## Part b(iii)

For a speed of $1000\,\mathrm{rpm}$ and rated motor current,

Determine the supply power factor.

### Solution

Use true power factor and ideal converter power balance:

$$
\mathrm{pf}=\frac{P_{ac}}{V_sI_{s,\mathrm{rms}}},\qquad P_{ac}=V_0I_0.
$$

With $V_0=55\,\mathrm V$ and $I_{s,\mathrm{rms}}=I_0$ from b(ii), cancel the current:

$$
\mathrm{pf}=\frac{55(33.909091)}{120(33.909091)}=\frac{55}{120}=\boxed{0.458333\quad\text{lagging displacement}}.
$$

### Source correction

The source's “$\approx0.5$” is a coarse approximation. Neither the true power factor $0.458333$ nor the displacement factor $\cos\alpha=0.509080$ should be silently substituted for the other. The square-wave current distortion factor is $2\sqrt2/\pi$.

## Part b(iv)

For a speed of $1000\,\mathrm{rpm}$ and rated motor current,

Draw waveforms of the supply voltage, supply current, converter output voltage, and converter output current.

### Solution

Apply the alternating conducting-pair schedule from part (a). Let $\theta=\omega t$, $v=\sqrt2(120)\sin\theta$, $I_0=33.909091\,\mathrm A$, and $\alpha=59.397409^\circ$. The conducting pair stays on for $\pi\,\text{radians}$ because the inductance maintains continuous positive load current:

$$
\sigma(\theta)=\begin{cases}+1,&\alpha\le\theta<\pi+\alpha,\\-1,&\pi+\alpha\le\theta<2\pi+\alpha,\end{cases}\qquad \sigma(\theta+2\pi)=\sigma(\theta).
$$

![Continuous-current switching and the four waveforms](figure:p2-f26-q5-waveforms)

The source voltage is sinusoidal, source current reverses only at firing instants, and the output voltage has negative portions while the previous pair remains conducting. The revealed plot implements

$$
\boxed{v=169.705627\sin\theta\,\mathrm V,\quad i=\sigma I_0,\quad v_0=\sigma v,\quad i_0=33.909091\,\mathrm A}.
$$
