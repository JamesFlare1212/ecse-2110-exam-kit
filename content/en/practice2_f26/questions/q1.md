## Question

A permanent magnet dc motor drives a mechanical load requiring a constant torque of $25\,\mathrm N\cdot\mathrm m$. The motor produces $10\,\mathrm N\cdot\mathrm m$ with an armature current of $10\,\mathrm A$. The resistance of the armature circuit is $0.2\,\Omega$. A $200\,\mathrm V$ dc supply is applied to the armature terminals. Determine the speed of the motor.

## Guide

Use the measured torque/current pair to find the constant magnetic coupling. The required load torque fixes the armature current; subtract its resistive voltage drop before converting back emf to speed.

## Part a

Determine the speed of the motor.

### Solution

Use the reference sheet's [DC torque relation](formula:dc-torque) and [generated-voltage relation](formula:dc-emf):

$$
T=K_a\Phi I_a,\qquad E_a=K_a\Phi\omega_m.
$$

The permanent magnet fixes $K_a\Phi$. The given calibration is $10\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ at $10\,\mathrm A$; at steady speed the developed torque equals the $25\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ load torque, neglecting unspecified rotational losses:

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_a\Phi&=\frac{10}{10}=1\,\mathrm{N\,m/A}=1\,\mathrm{V\,s/rad},\\
I_a&=\frac{25}{1}=25\,\mathrm A.
\end{aligned}
$$

The solution circuit below represents the armature by a series resistance $R_a$ and an internal back emf $E_a$. Current $I_a$ leaves the positive supply terminal, crosses the resistor from left to right and enters the positive terminal of the back-emf element. The back emf is induced by rotation, not a second external supply; in motor operation it opposes the supply's drive of current. At steady speed and current, the inductive voltage is zero.

![Armature circuit and KVL traversal](figure:p2-f26-q1-armature)

Kirchhoff's voltage law states that the algebraic sum of potential changes around a closed loop is zero. Start at the negative supply terminal and traverse clockwise: crossing the supply from $-$ to $+$ gives a rise $+V$; crossing the resistor with the current gives a drop $-I_aR_a$; crossing the back-emf element from $+$ to $-$ gives a drop $-E_a$. Ideal connecting wires contribute no drop. Hence

$$
+V-I_aR_a-E_a=0,\qquad E_a=V-I_aR_a.
$$

The $200\,\mathrm V$ supply therefore covers the resistive drop and the back emf; the entire $200\,\mathrm V$ is not across the resistance. Substitute $I_a=25\,\mathrm A$ and $R_a=0.2\,\Omega$, then use the generated-voltage relation to find speed:

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_aR_a&=25(0.2)=5\,\mathrm V,\\
E_a&=200-5=195\,\mathrm V,\\
\omega_m&=\frac{E_a}{K_a\Phi}=\frac{195}{1}=195\,\mathrm{rad/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Convert radians per second to revolutions per minute:

$$
n=\frac{60\omega_m}{2\pi}=\frac{60(195)}{2\pi}=\boxed{1862.11\,\mathrm{rpm}}.
$$
