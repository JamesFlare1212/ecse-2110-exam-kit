## Question

A $60\text{-}\mathrm{MVA}$, $69.3\text{-}\mathrm{kV}$, three-phase synchronous generator has a synchronous reactance of $15\,\Omega$/phase and negligible armature resistance.

## Guide

Use the per-phase generator model with outward current. Resolve the rated current into real and imaginary components for the first request, use the power-angle limit for the second, and find the stable power angle before recovering the current in the third.

## Part a

The generator is delivering rated power at $0.8$ power factor lagging at the rated terminal voltage to an infinite bus bar. Determine the magnitude of the generated emf per phase and the power angle $\delta$.

### Solution

Use the [generator current relation](formula:generator-current), rearranged for generated emf, and the balanced three-phase apparent-power identity from $S=\sum V_\phi I_\phi^*$ (complex-power definition, [Lecture 2, PDF page 13](/pages/lecture-02/13.webp)):

$$
E=V+jX_sI_a,\qquad |S|=3V|I_a|,\qquad \theta=\cos^{-1}(0.8).
$$

Take $V$ as the real reference and interpret “rated power” as the stated $60\,\mathrm{MVA}$ rating. The lagging current has negative imaginary component:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=\frac{69300}{\sqrt3}=40010.373655\,\mathrm V,\\
|I_a|&=\frac{60\times10^6}{3(40010.373655)}=499.870363\,\mathrm A,\\
I_a&=499.870363(0.8-j0.6)=399.896290-j299.922218\,\mathrm A,\\
E&=40010.373655+j15(399.896290-j299.922218)\\
 &=44509.206921+j5998.444355\,\mathrm V.
\end{aligned}
$$

Take the magnitude and the positive generator angle:

$$
\begin{aligned}
|E|&=\sqrt{44509.206921^2+5998.444355^2}=\boxed{44.911589\,\mathrm{kV/phase}},\\
\delta&=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{5998.444355}{44509.206921}\right)=7.675428^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

## Part b

If the generated emf is $36\,\mathrm{kV}$ per phase, what is the maximum three-phase power that the generator can deliver before losing its synchronism?

### Solution

Use the reference sheet's [power-angle relation](formula:power-angle):

$$
P=\frac{3EV}{X_s}\sin\delta.
$$

For the specified $E=36000\,\mathrm V$, fixed bus phase voltage $V=69300/\sqrt3\,\mathrm V$, and $X_s=15\,\Omega$, the ideal sinusoidal characteristic reaches its maximum at $\delta=90^\circ$:

$$
\frac{dP}{d\delta}=\frac{3EV}{X_s}\cos\delta=0,\qquad \sin90^\circ=1.
$$

Substitute the actual phase voltage:

$$
P_{\max}=\frac{3(36000)(69300/\sqrt3)}{15}=\boxed{288.074690\,\mathrm{MW}\quad(\delta=90^\circ)}.
$$

### Source correction

The source's $288\,\mathrm{MW}$ uses a rounded $40\,\mathrm{kV}$ phase voltage. This is the ideal pull-out boundary, with zero synchronizing slope, not an operating point with stability margin. It does not waive the machine's thermal/current rating; $288\,\mathrm{MW}$ greatly exceeds its $60\,\mathrm{MVA}$ rating.

## Part c

The generator is delivering $48\,\mathrm{MW}$ to the bus bar at the rated voltage with its field current adjusted for a generated emf of $46\,\mathrm{kV}$ per phase. Determine the armature current and the power factor. State whether the power factor is lagging or leading?

### Solution

Use the [power-angle relation](formula:power-angle) and [generator current relation](formula:generator-current):

$$
\sin\delta=\frac{PX_s}{3EV},\qquad I_a=\frac{E\angle\delta-V}{jX_s}.
$$

Given $P=48\times10^6\,\mathrm W$, $E=46000\,\mathrm V$, $V=40010.373655\,\mathrm V$, and $X_s=15\,\Omega$, choose the stable branch $0<\delta<90^\circ$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta&=\sin^{-1}\!\left(\frac{48\times10^6(15)}{3(46000)(40010.373655)}\right)=7.492763^\circ,\\
I_a&=\frac{46000(\cos7.492763^\circ+j\sin7.492763^\circ)-40010.373655}{j15}\\
&=399.896290-j373.123199\,\mathrm A.
\end{aligned}
$$

The negative current angle means the outward current lags the terminal voltage. Hence

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_a&=\boxed{546.935064\angle-43.016386^\circ\,\mathrm A},\\
\mathrm{pf}&=\frac{399.896290}{\sqrt{399.896290^2+373.123199^2}}=0.731159\quad\text{lagging}.
\end{aligned}
$$

### Source correction

The source writes the power factor as $\cos^{-1}43.06=0.7306$; power factor is the cosine of the current angle, not its inverse cosine. The numerical differences also reflect its rounded phase voltage and current arithmetic. The high-angle solution $180^\circ-7.492763^\circ$ is outside the usual stable operating branch.
