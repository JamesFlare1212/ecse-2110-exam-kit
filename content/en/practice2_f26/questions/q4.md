## Question

Consider the single-phase ac–dc converter shown in Fig. P10.4. The SCRs are triggered alternately at an interval of $180^\circ$. Assume a very large inductance in the dc circuit.

## Guide

Trace each current path in the actual two-SCR, two-diode bridge. The diode leg carries freewheeling current between source-conduction intervals. Integrate only the intervals where the source supplies the load; the half-controlled bridge does not obey the full-controlled-bridge voltage formula.

## Source correction

Editorial question 4 is the prompt on PDF page 5; its solution continues on page 6. The source diagram is reproduced with its original leg order and labels. No marks are printed. Ideal switches and continuous DC current are assumed. Device currents are positive in their forward conduction directions; $v_{s1}$ is the anode-to-cathode voltage of $S_1$.

## Part a

Draw the waveforms of $i_0$, $v_0$, $i_{s1}$, $i_{s2}$, $i_{d1}$, $i_{d2}$, $i$, and $v_{s1}$ for a firing angle $\alpha=30^\circ$.

### Solution

Use ideal-switch conduction and Kirchhoff's laws on the supplied bridge. Let $\theta=\omega t$, $v=V_m\sin\theta$, and $I_0$ be the constant load current. Over $0\le\theta<2\pi$, the conduction paths are

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\theta&\text{conducting devices}&v_0\\\hline
[0,\alpha)&D_1,D_2&0\\
[\alpha,\pi)&S_1,D_2&v\\
[\pi,\pi+\alpha)&D_1,D_2&0\\
[\pi+\alpha,2\pi)&S_2,D_1&-v
\end{array}
$$

![Conduction states and converter waveforms](figure:p2-f26-q4-waveforms)

The diode labels matter: in the drawing $D_1$ is the upper right diode and $D_2$ the lower right diode. Thus the positive-source path uses $S_1,D_2$, not $S_1,D_1$. Substitute $\alpha=\pi/6$ to locate every edge in the revealed plot. $D_1$ carries $I_0$ except during $[\alpha,\pi)$; $D_2$ carries $I_0$ except during $[\pi+\alpha,2\pi)$. The thyristors conduct only on their respective source intervals. With the bottom bus as reference, the resulting waveform values are

$$
\boxed{\begin{gathered}
i_0=I_0,\qquad
(v_0,i)=\begin{cases}(0,0),&0\le\theta<\pi/6,\\(v,I_0),&\pi/6\le\theta<\pi,\\(0,0),&\pi\le\theta<7\pi/6,\\(-v,-I_0),&7\pi/6\le\theta<2\pi,\end{cases}\\
v_{s1}=\begin{cases}v,&0\le\theta<\pi/6,\\0,&\pi/6\le\theta<\pi,\\v,&\pi\le\theta<2\pi,\end{cases}\qquad\text{repeat every }2\pi.
\end{gathered}}
$$

### Source correction

The handwritten source labels the positive path $S_1,D_1$ and negative path $S_2,D_2$, reversing the diode labels of its own circuit. During $[\pi,\pi+\alpha)$, freewheeling clamps both DC buses to the second AC terminal, so $S_1$ already has reverse voltage $v<0$. The original voltage trace correctly starts this reverse voltage at $\pi$; the revealed plot retains that behavior while correcting the diode-current labels.

## Part b

If $V=208\,\mathrm V$, $R=10\,\Omega$, $L\to\infty$, and $\alpha=90^\circ$, find the average output current ($I_0$), the rms supply current ($I$), and the supply power factor.

### Solution

Derive the semi-converter average from the actual source-conduction intervals in part (a); the two equal pulses give

$$
V_0=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_\alpha^\pi V_m\sin\theta\,d\theta,\qquad V_m=\sqrt2V.
$$

Take the constant $V_m$ outside the integral, find the antiderivative, and evaluate the endpoints:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_0&=\frac{V_m}{\pi}[-\cos\theta]_\alpha^\pi
=\frac{V_m}{\pi}\bigl[-\cos\pi-(-\cos\alpha)\bigr]\\
&=\frac{\sqrt2V}{\pi}(1+\cos\alpha)
=\frac{\sqrt2(208)}{\pi}(1+\cos90^\circ)=93.632897\,\mathrm V,\\
I_0&=\frac{V_0}{R}=\frac{93.632897}{10}=9.363290\,\mathrm A.
\end{aligned}
$$

The source current is zero during freewheeling and has magnitude $I_0$ for total angle $2(\pi-\alpha)$ per cycle. Evaluate its mean square explicitly:

$$
\begin{aligned}
I^2&=\frac{2}{2\pi}\int_\alpha^\pi I_0^2\,d\theta
=\frac{I_0^2}{\pi}[\theta]_\alpha^\pi
=I_0^2\frac{\pi-\alpha}{\pi},\\
I&=9.363290\sqrt{\frac{\pi-\pi/2}{\pi}}=6.620846\,\mathrm A,\\
P&=I_0^2R=(9.363290)^2(10)=876.711938\,\mathrm W.
\end{aligned}
$$

The ideal converter has no loss, so its true input power factor is

$$
\boxed{I_0=9.363290\,\mathrm A,\quad I=6.620846\,\mathrm A,\quad
\mathrm{pf}=\frac{876.711938}{208(6.620846)}=0.636620}.
$$

### Source correction

This relation is derived for the half-controlled circuit, not attributed to the reference sheet's resistive half-wave or full-converter formula. The source's $0.637$ and other small numerical discrepancies result from intermediate rounding.

## Part c

What is the range of the firing angle for this converter?

### Solution

An SCR can be fired only while forward biased. Refer the positive SCR's delay to the positive supply zero crossing, and trigger the other one $\pi\,\text{radians}$ later:

$$
0\le\alpha\le\pi,\qquad \theta_{S_2}=\theta_{S_1}+\pi.
$$

A nonzero source-conduction interval requires $\pi-\alpha>0$. The lower endpoint gives the uncontrolled-bridge limit; the upper endpoint has no delivered DC voltage and cannot sustain positive current in a resistive load:

$$
\boxed{0\le\alpha\le180^\circ\ \text{as ideal limits};\quad 0<\alpha<180^\circ\ \text{for delayed, nonzero rectification}}.
$$

## Part d

Is inversion possible with this converter? Explain.

### Solution

Use the average voltage derived in (b), with the diode-enforced load-current direction:

$$
V_0=\frac{V_m}{\pi}(1+\cos\alpha),\qquad I_0\ge0.
$$

On $0\le\alpha\le\pi$, the factor $1+\cos\alpha$ lies between zero and two. The freewheeling diode path prevents a sustained negative output voltage; consequently

$$
\boxed{V_0\ge0,\quad P_{dc}=V_0I_0\ge0;\qquad \text{line-commutated inversion is impossible}}.
$$
