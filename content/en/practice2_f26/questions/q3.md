## Question

The speed of a dc motor is controlled by a $1ϕ$ ac–dc full converter, as shown in Fig. 10.21. The ac supply is $1ϕ$, $120\,\mathrm V$, $60\,\mathrm{Hz}$. The armature resistance is $R_a=1\,\Omega$ and armature circuit inductance is $L_a=25\,\mathrm{mH}$. The motor voltage constant is $0.055\,\mathrm{V/rpm}$. The field current of the motor is adjusted such that the armature voltage $E_a$ is negative. The firing angle is $\alpha=60^\circ$, and the speed of the dc machine is $200\,\mathrm{rpm}$. Assume that the dc machine current is continuous and ripple-free.

## Guide

Keep the signed emf separate from its magnitude. With negative emf and positive current, the machine converts mechanical power to electricity while the AC supply also feeds the resistor. Use true input power factor, including the square-wave current's distortion.

## Part a

Determine the average value ($I_0$) for the dc machine current.

### Solution

Use the reference sheet's [full-converter average voltage](formula:rectifier-full), also shown on [Lecture 11–12, PDF page 24](/pages/lecture-11-12/24.webp):

$$
V_0=\frac{2\sqrt2V_s}{\pi}\cos\alpha.
$$

To find the mean current $I_0$, also establish the relation between terminal voltage and armature current. This is not another reference-sheet formula: it follows from Kirchhoff's voltage law (KVL) applied to the series armature branch in [Fig. 10.21 on source PDF page 9](/papers/en/practice2_f26/pages/p2-f26-sol/9.webp), the matching source for this question's omitted figure. Reference $v_0(t)$ positive at the upper armature terminal, take $i_0(t)$ downward through $L_a$, $R_a$ and $E_a$, and keep the emf reference fixed at upper $+$ and lower $-$, as in the solution diagram below.

![Solution-only full bridge and signed-emf armature, not a reconstructed prompt diagram](figure:p2-f26-q3-circuit)

With these references, the terminal voltage contributes a rise $+v_0$. Traversing the inductor, resistor and internal emf with the current gives potential changes $-L_a\,di_0/dt$, $-R_ai_0$ and $-E_a$. Their algebraic sum around the loop is zero:

$$
\begin{aligned}
+v_0(t)-L_a\frac{di_0}{dt}-R_ai_0(t)-E_a&=0,\\
v_0(t)&=E_a+R_ai_0(t)+L_a\frac{di_0}{dt}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Let $T=1/f$ be the supply period, with $V_0$ and $I_0$ the period averages of $v_0(t)$ and $i_0(t)$. Fixed speed and excitation make $E_a$ constant, and $R_a$ and $L_a$ are constant circuit parameters. Periodic steady state requires $i_0(T)=i_0(0)$. Integrate term by term and evaluate the endpoints:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_0&=\frac1T\int_0^T\left(E_a+R_ai_0(t)+L_a\frac{di_0}{dt}\right)dt\\
&=\frac{E_a}{T}[t]_0^T+\frac{R_a}{T}\int_0^T i_0(t)\,dt+\frac{L_a}{T}[i_0(t)]_0^T\\
&=\frac{E_a}{T}(T-0)+R_aI_0+\frac{L_a}{T}\bigl[i_0(T)-i_0(0)\bigr]\\
&=E_a+R_aI_0.
\end{aligned}
$$

It is the mean inductor voltage that vanishes; the given $L_a=25\,\mathrm{mH}$ has not been set to zero. Nor does this make the instantaneous terminal voltage $v_0(t)$ equal to its mean $V_0$ at every instant. We retain the question's ripple-free-current idealization for the averaged model.

The proportionality between generated voltage and speed is shown on [Lecture 9, PDF page 8](/pages/lecture-09/8.webp). Here the given $k_n$ expresses that proportionality per revolution per minute. The minus sign comes from the stated $E_a<0$, not from reversing the drawn voltage reference. Thus

$$
E_a=-k_nn,\qquad I_0=\frac{V_0-E_a}{R_a}.
$$

The given voltage constant is per $\mathrm{rpm}$, so insert $n=200\,\mathrm{rpm}$ directly, without converting it to radians per second:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_0&=\frac{2\sqrt2(120)}{\pi}\cos60^\circ=54.018979\,\mathrm V,\\
E_a&=-0.055(200)=-11\,\mathrm V,\\
I_0&=\frac{54.018979-(-11)}{1}=\boxed{65.018979\,\mathrm A}.
\end{aligned}
$$

## Part b

Is the dc machine delivering power or receiving power? Determine this power ($P_a$).

### Solution

Use the reference sheet's [converted machine power](formula:mechanical-power), with positive electrical absorption defined by current entering the positive-emf reference terminal:

$$
P_{a,\mathrm{abs}}=E_aI_0.
$$

Part (a) gives $E_a=-11\,\mathrm V$ and $I_0=65.018979\,\mathrm A$. Their product is negative, so the machine delivers converted power into the electrical circuit:

$$
P_{a,\mathrm{del}}=-(-11)(65.018979)=\boxed{715.208769\,\mathrm W\quad\text{delivered}}.
$$

## Part c

Is the ac supply receiving power, or delivering power? Determine this power ($P_{ac}$).

### Solution

For an ideal lossless converter, AC input power equals DC output power. The ripple-free current makes the mean product equal to $V_0I_0$:

$$
P_{ac,\mathrm{del}}=\langle v_0i_0\rangle=V_0I_0.
$$

Use part (a)'s positive voltage and current. Positive power flows from the supply through the converter:

$$
P_{ac,\mathrm{del}}=(54.018979)(65.018979)=\boxed{3512.258858\,\mathrm W\quad\text{delivered}}.
$$

## Part d

Where is all the power going?

### Solution

Joule's law gives the armature copper loss; an ideal inductor has zero net stored-energy change over a period:

$$
P_R=I_{\mathrm{rms}}^2R_a,\qquad \langle p_L\rangle=0.
$$

The imposed ripple-free current means $I_{\mathrm{rms}}=I_0=65.018979\,\mathrm A$. Compare resistor heating with the two delivered powers:

$$
P_R=(65.018979)^2(1)=3512.258858+715.208769=\boxed{4227.467626\,\mathrm W\quad\text{armature heating}}.
$$

### Source correction

Both the AC source and mechanically driven machine deliver power. Their sum is dissipated in $R_a$; this operating point is not regeneration into the AC supply. The source's $4225\,\mathrm W$ and other whole-number results round $V_0$ to $54\,\mathrm V$ first.

## Part e

Determine the supply power factor.

### Solution

Use the definition of true power factor, rather than only the fundamental displacement angle:

$$
\mathrm{pf}=\frac{P_{ac}}{V_sI_{s,\mathrm{rms}}}.
$$

The ideal full bridge draws $+I_0$ or $-I_0$ continuously, so $I_{s,\mathrm{rms}}=I_0$. Substituting $P_{ac}=V_0I_0$ cancels the current:

$$
\mathrm{pf}=\frac{V_0I_0}{120I_0}=\frac{54.018979}{120}=\boxed{0.450158\quad\text{lagging displacement}}.
$$
