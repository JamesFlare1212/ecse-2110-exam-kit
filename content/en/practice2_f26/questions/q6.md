## Question

A 1ϕ ac–dc full converter is used to control the speed of a dc motor, as shown in Fig. 10.21. The motor parameters are $R_a=0.25\,\Omega$, $k_a\Phi=0.1\,\mathrm{V/rpm}$, and $L_a$ is sufficiently large. The converter is fed from a 1ϕ, 120 V, 60 Hz supply. For $\alpha=60^\circ$, the motor current is continuous, and motor average current is 20 A, and the ripple current is $20\%$.

## Guide

Use average current in the mean armature voltage equation and converted power. Use total RMS current in copper loss and supply apparent power. A ripple component contributes extra heating, so average output power is not generally the product of average voltage and average current.

## Part a

Draw waveforms of supply voltage ($v$), supply current ($i$), output voltage ($v_0$), and output current ($i_0$).

### Solution

Use the continuous-current switching schedule of the four-SCR bridge (Lecture 11–12, PDF page 23). With $\theta=\omega t$, the positive pair $S_1,S_2$ conducts from $\alpha$ to $\pi+\alpha$, and $S_3,S_4$ conducts for the next $\pi$ radians:

$$
\sigma(\theta)=\begin{cases}+1,&\pi/3\le\theta<4\pi/3,\\-1,&4\pi/3\le\theta<7\pi/3,\end{cases}\qquad \sigma(\theta+2\pi)=\sigma(\theta).
$$

The stated RMS ripple factor fixes $\langle\widetilde i\rangle=0$ and $\sqrt{\langle\widetilde i^2\rangle}=0.20(20)=4\,\mathrm A$, but does not by itself prescribe a time-domain ripple shape without a load-dynamics model. The revealed current curve is a representative periodic RL–emf solution chosen to meet those values; its shape is an educational solution illustration, not another given. For any physically consistent ripple waveform, the required switching relationships are

$$
\boxed{\begin{gathered}
v=169.705627\sin\theta\,\mathrm V,\quad v_0=\sigma v,\\
i_0=20+\widetilde i(\theta)>0,\quad i=\sigma i_0,\quad
\langle\widetilde i\rangle=0,\quad \widetilde I_{\mathrm{rms}}=4\,\mathrm A.
\end{gathered}}
$$

### Source correction

Use the circuit's diagonal pairs $S_1,S_2$ and $S_3,S_4$, switching supply-current sign at $\alpha$ and $\pi+\alpha$. Exact ripple shape is not a separately printed given. Finite ripple must not be replaced with a flat current when calculating resistor losses.

## Part b

Determine the speed of the motor in rpm.

### Solution

Use the [full-converter voltage](formula:rectifier-full) and the mean armature loop equation. Periodic inductor current makes $\langle L_a\,di_0/dt\rangle=0$:

$$
V_0=\frac{2\sqrt2V_s}{\pi}\cos\alpha=E_a+R_aI_0,\qquad E_a=k_nn.
$$

Insert $V_s=120\,\mathrm V$, $\alpha=60^\circ$, $I_0=20\,\mathrm A$, $R_a=0.25\,\Omega$ and the given constant in V/rpm:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_0&=\frac{2\sqrt2(120)}{\pi}\cos60^\circ=54.018979\,\mathrm V,\\
E_a&=54.018979-0.25(20)=49.018979\,\mathrm V,\\
n&=\frac{49.018979}{0.1}=\boxed{490.189790\,\mathrm{rpm}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

## Part c

Determine the power lost in the armature resistance and the power developed by the motor.

### Solution

Use Joule heating with total RMS current and [converted machine power](formula:mechanical-power). For constant back emf and periodic ripple with zero mean,

$$
I_{0,\mathrm{rms}}^2=I_0^2+I_{\sim,\mathrm{rms}}^2,\qquad P_R=R_aI_{0,\mathrm{rms}}^2,\qquad P_{\mathrm{dev}}=E_aI_0.
$$

Given $I_0=20\,\mathrm A$ and $r=0.20$, the ripple RMS is $4\,\mathrm A$. Use the emf from (b):

$$
I_{0,\mathrm{rms}}=\sqrt{20^2+4^2}=\sqrt{416}=20.396078\,\mathrm A.
$$

Calculate the requested powers:

$$
\boxed{P_R=0.25(20^2+4^2)=104.000000\,\mathrm W,\qquad P_{\mathrm{dev}}=49.018979(20)=980.379579\,\mathrm W}.
$$

### Source correction

The handwritten answer rounds RMS current to 20.4 A and obtains 105 W. Retaining the given $20\%$ RMS ripple gives exactly 104 W. Developed power uses average current, while resistor heating uses RMS current.

## Part d

Determine the supply power factor.

### Solution

Use true input power factor and average energy conservation for an ideal converter and periodic inductor:

$$
\mathrm{pf}=\frac{P_{ac}}{V_sI_{s,\mathrm{rms}}},\qquad P_{ac}=P_R+P_{\mathrm{dev}},\qquad I_{s,\mathrm{rms}}=I_{0,\mathrm{rms}}.
$$

Part (c) gives $P_R=104\,\mathrm W$, $P_{\mathrm{dev}}=980.379579\,\mathrm W$ and $I_{s,\mathrm{rms}}=\sqrt{416}\,\mathrm A$. Thus

$$
\mathrm{pf}=\frac{104+980.379579}{120\sqrt{416}}=\boxed{0.443051}.
$$

### Source correction

With ripple, $\langle v_0i_0\rangle\ne V_0I_0$: here the difference is $R_aI_{\sim,\mathrm{rms}}^2=0.25(4^2)=4$ W. The mean product is 1084.379579 W, while the product of means is 1080.379579 W. Using the latter for input power would violate energy conservation by omitting ripple heating. The source's 1085 W and 0.443 are rounded versions of its loss-plus-converted-power method.
