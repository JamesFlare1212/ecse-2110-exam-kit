Main Types of Synchronous Generators:

There are two types of synchronous generators (machines):
➢Round-rotor:
   ❑They have a cylindrical rotor, a uniform air gap and one distributed
       winding.
   ❑These generators are driven by steam turbines and are designed for high
      speed performance.
    ❑Roughly 70 percent of large synchronous generators are cylindrical rotor
        type. (In USA)
➢Salient pole:
   ❑These kind of generators have concentrated windings on the poles and
       non-uniform air gaps.
   ❑They have a relatively large number of poles, short axial length, and large
       diameter.
   ❑They are designed for lower speed performances, such a hydraulic
        turbines.

            Rotor Damper Windings



➢Damping windings (amortisseurs) are used in salient pole machines to improve dynamic performance:
   ❑The windings function as squirrel cage–type windings which are located in the slots of the poles.
    ❑Unlike the field winding there is no DC excitation and they are short-circuited.
   ❑The short-circuiting can be separately around each pole-piece or connected around the rotor (see above).


➢During steady state operation, the effect of damper windings is typically negligible because they are rotating with
   synchronous speed.
   ❑The induced currents in the damper windings are only due to spatial harmonics of the rotating field with frequencies 1/5, 1/7,
        …, of the synchronous frequency.
   ❑The currents that float through them helps to eliminate these small-amplitude harmonics.
➢During faults or non-synchronous speed operation (oscillations) the EMF from these windings opposes that of the
   source (fault or oscillations).
   ❑The result is a damping torque, which allows the current flow to return to steady state quicker.
