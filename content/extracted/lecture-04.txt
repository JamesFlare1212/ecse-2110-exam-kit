              Eddy Current losses
➢Ideally, flux produced by one winding should flow through the core of the
  transformer and link all turns of the other winding, but:
   ❑Some flux fails to link winding turns
   ❑Flux can fringe at core edges and gaps, causing the field to be non-uniform


➢Eddy currents: time-varying magnetic flux induces eddy currents in
  conductors (Lentz’s law) that oppose the change in flux that causes the eddy
   currents


➢Core is composed of sheets insulated from each other to confine eddy
   currents to individual sheets

                Using Ampere’s law



➢If the primary winding has N1 turns, then current I1 is enclosed N1 times (once
  for each turn of the winding), and I2 is enclosed N2 times.
➢Using the right-hand rule, I1 contributes to clockwise flux, and I2 to the
  counterclockwise flux.
➢Therefore, the NET current enclosed is:
  N1I1 – N2I2

--- PAGE 25 ---
                Using Faraday’s law


➢Voltage v(t) induced across a winding of N turns by a time-varying flux ϕ(t) is:
   v(t) = Ndϕ(t)/dt or, in sinusoidal steady-state, V = NjωΦ

➢Ideal transformer: entire flux, Φ, is confined to the core, i.e., Φc fully links both the
  primary and secondary windings

➢Therefore V1 = N1jωΦc and V2 = N2jωΦc

➢From this, we have: V1/N1 = V2/N2

                    Turns ratio

--- PAGE 27 ---
Reflecting impedances through an ideal transformer


      ➢An ideal transformer supplies a load impedance ZL, i.e., V2 = ZLI2  or ZL = V2/I2

      ➢In the primary side, V1 = ZL’I1

         ➢Substituting for V2 = V1/at and I2 = atI1, we have:
                ZL = (V1/at)/(atI1) = V1/I1)(1/at2) = ZL’(1/at2)

       ➢Or: ZL’ (load impedance viewed from primary) = a2ZL

       ➢That is, a load impedance connected to the secondary of the transformer,
           appears as the load impedance multiplied by turn ratio squared when viewed
           from the primary side

       ➢This way, we can simplify calculations that involve transformers. We will
            develop an even simpler method.
