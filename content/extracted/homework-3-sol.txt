Question 1. (10 points) Explain about magnetic saturation, magnetic inertia, hysteresis and
eddy currents.

Answer:

Magnetic Saturation: When a magnetic core (material) is subjected to an external magnetic
field that is increasing, then at one point the magnetization of the core is not proportional to the
increasing external field. This state is called magnetic saturation. Magnetic saturation occurs due
to the fact that most of the magnetic dipoles inside the core material are already aligned to the
external field.

Magnetic Inertia: When a magnetic core (material) is subjected to an external magnetic field
that is increasing, the initial magnetization of the core is a little sluggish due to magnetic inertia
meaning that the magnetic dipoles in the core initially resist to orient in the direction of external
field.

Hysteresis: If the external magnetic field is removed then a small portion of magnetic field is
retained by the core. This needs to be overcome for every magnetic reversal when AC magnetic
flux is setup in the core called hysteresis.

Eddy currents: When a AC voltage is applied across an electromagnet or to the primary
winding of a transformer, then the currents are induced in the core material called Eddy currents.
This is due to Faraday’s law.


Question 2. (10 points) How to mitigate Hysteresis and eddy current losses in a
transformer?

Answer:

Hysteresis losses can be minimized by choosing a material with low magnetic remanence and
Eddy current losses can be minimized using a laminated core.


Question 3. (10 Points) Describe about three-phase balanced voltages and three-phase
balanced loads.

Answer:

A 3-phase voltage is called balanced when all three phase voltage magnitudes are equal and they
are 1200 apart in phase from each other. A 3-phase load is called balance load if same
impedances are present in all three phases.
