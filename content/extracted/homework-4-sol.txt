Question 1. (20 points)

    a.  (5 points) Explain about the losses in a transformer in detail.
      Answer:
    A transformer has electrical losses and magnetic losses. Electrical losses occur in primary
       as well as in secondary windings due to their electrical resistance. These are the ohmic
        losses that depend on the load current of the transformer. The magnetic losses are of two
        types, Hysteresis and eddy current losses that are independent of load current. Hysteresis
        losses occur due to the power loss to overcome the magnetic remanence during magnetic
        reversals. Eddy current losses are the ohmic losses in the magnetic core due to induction
       of eddy currents in the core. The core material also satisfies Faraday’s law during the
       regular operation of a transformer.


    b.  (5 points) How to minimize hysteresis and eddy current losses in a transformer?
      Answer:
       Hysteresis loss can be minimized by selecting the core material with less retentivity (low
      remanence) whereas the eddy current loss can be minimized by using a laminated core as
      opposed to a solid core.


    c.  (5 points) What are in-rush currents in a transformer?
      Answer:
     When a transformer is energized from a power supply, then a large current can flow into
       the transformer, called in-rush current, since there is no opposition to the applied voltage.
      The magnitude of this current is a function of the point on wave when voltage is applied.


    d. (5 points) Why calculate all-day efficiency of a transformer?

   Answer:

   All-day efficiency of a transfer represents more accurate efficiency of a transformer that
    takes into account of the daily load cycle on the transformer.
