            The “system’s frequency”


➢ The electrical frequency is related to the generator speed, which can
   measured at the shaft of the generator.
➢  Keeping the frequency constant (or nearly constant) at a 50 or 60 Hz
   reference is needed because:
   ❑  AC machines (generators and motors) rotate in AC synchrony
   ❑  Machines are designed at operating frequency, where they have maximum
           efficiency, and large deviations can lead to unnecessary wear and/or
        damage.
   ❑  The electrical power output is a function of the machines’ speeds


➢ The frequency is thus kept within specific limits (or regulation bands).
➢ However, it can not be measured at the substations.
➢  Frequency estimates at any node/bus/substation in the network can be
   obtained using PMUs and other devices.
   ❑  The instantaneous frequency is different at different power system locations
                                                                                         Note! The frequency
   ❑  However, the average of frequencies at different locations preserve – system-

                                                                                            constant (50/60 Hz)!➢ Power system controls, such as a speed governor and Automatic
   Generation Controls (AGC) are used to minimize deviations in the

--- PAGE 3 ---
       A “power system” – a balancing act!

➢A simple power system puts together all the components
  above in an ensemble that:
   ❑Operates in AC synchrony (rotating machinery at the same
       speed)
    ❑Continuously attempts to meet all consumption (loads + losses)
       with electrical power supply (generators)
    ❑Electrical energy produce goes through transformers,
       transmission lines. …
   ❑Generation = load + losses
➢And an interconnected power system differs in that
   ❑Attempts to meet meet consumption + losses + exchanges =
      generation
➢Exchanges are imports or exports from another power
  network outside the “operational boundary”
➢The system’s synchrony reflects to what extent the above
  relationships are met.
    ❑Deviations in the machines’ speed reflects a change
        in generated/consumed power.

--- PAGE 14 ---
Conservation of Power

✓At every node (bus) in the system
    • Sum of real power injected into node must equal zero.
    • Sum of reactive power injected into node must equal zero.

✓This is a direct consequence of Kirchhoff’s current law, which states that the
  total current into each node must equal zero.
    • Conservation of power follows since S = VI*

--- PAGE 18 ---
Power factor correction:
  ✓The larger current supplied in a circuit will be larger for PF<1 than it would be
     for PF=1.
   ✓It is suitable that major loads on the system have power factor as close to 1
     as possible.
   ✓This is done by installing local banks of capacitors throughout the network
    because majority of industrial loads are inductive.
   ✓Reactive compensation decreases the line flow. This has some advantages:
        • Less losses in lines.
        • Decreased current, allowing the use of thinner conductors, or, capability to supply
       more load over the same conductors.
        • Less voltage drop over the line.
